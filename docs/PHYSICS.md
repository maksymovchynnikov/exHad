# Physics of the exHad decay models

This document describes, for each model of the model table in the [README](../README.md),
the interaction that defines the decaying particle, the calculations and measurements that fix
its decay rates, and how exHad generates its hadronic and nonhadronic final states at each mass.
The mass ranges in which exHad generates decays are given in that table. How to request decays
and use the returned events is described in [INTERFACES.md](INTERFACES.md), and the exact
sampling algorithms in [SAMPLING.md](SAMPLING.md). *The scalar models* denotes `scalar-central`,
`scalar-lower`, `scalar-upper` and `scalar-1809`. The branching ratios and the generated events do not
depend on the overall size of the couplings defined in the interaction of each model; for `hnl`, they
depend on the ratios of the three squared mixings.

## Hadronic final states from constrained string fragmentation

exHad generates each hadronic decay either in a separately calculated exclusive channel or by
Pythia string fragmentation restricted by the quantum numbers of the decaying particle. Exclusive
calculations and measurements fix the channel rates near the lower end of each mass range. At
higher masses, fits to measured cross sections give the rates of `dark-photon`, and a common power
law in the squared mass of the hadronic system, Eq. (2), gives the probabilities of the channel
groups of the other models.

In a hadronic decay, the particle produces a quark–antiquark pair or a gluon pair, for example
$`A' \to u\bar u`$ or $`a \to gg`$. The two partons carry opposite colour charges and together form a
colourless state. [Pythia](https://pythia.org) 8.317
([source repository](https://gitlab.com/Pythia8/releases)) turns this state into hadrons with the
Lund string model: the colour field between the partons is a string, which breaks through the
creation of quark–antiquark pairs, and the pieces form hadrons (string fragmentation). *Unmodified
Pythia* denotes string fragmentation of the partons of the decay without further restrictions. Pythia's
default fragmentation parameters were determined mainly from $`e^+e^-`$ annihilation data at the high
energies of LEP and SLD. At hadronic masses of a few GeV, unmodified Pythia produces final states forbidden by the quantum
numbers of the decaying particle and assigns incorrect probabilities to the allowed final states.
The *constrained fragmentation model* of exHad adds three ingredients to string fragmentation:
exclusive decay rates, the quantum numbers of the current that produces the partons, and a common
mass dependence of the probabilities of multihadron final states. The subsections below define
them.

### Widths and channel groups

The total hadronic width is divided into the widths of separately generated exclusive channels
and the width generated by string fragmentation, and the latter is shared among channel groups.

Let $`\Gamma_{\rm tot}`$ be the total decay width and $`\Gamma_{\rm had}`$ the partial width into all
final states that contain hadrons, including hadrons with photons such as $`\pi^0\gamma`$. Decays
into leptons, neutrinos and photon pairs contribute to $`\Gamma_{\rm tot}`$ separately. An
*exclusive channel* is a decay into a fixed set of hadrons, such as $`\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$. exHad
classifies a final state after the decays of short-lived resonances such as $`\rho`$, $`\omega`$,
$`\phi`$ and $`K^\ast`$, keeping pions, kaons, $`\eta`$, $`\eta'`$, photons and nucleons undecayed at this
stage. For example, $`\omega\pi^+\pi^-`$ followed by $`\omega\to\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$ is the final state
$`2(\pi^+\pi^-)\pi^0`$, and both $`K^{\ast 0}K^-\pi^+`$ followed by $`K^{\ast 0}\to K^+\pi^-`$ and
$`\phi\pi^+\pi^-`$ followed by $`\phi\to K^+K^-`$ are the final state $`K^+K^-\pi^+\pi^-`$.

Some channels have partial widths from a separate calculation or measurement, for example decays
into two mesons or into a nucleon and an antinucleon. exHad generates their final states without
string fragmentation; they are called *separately generated channels* below. Let
$`\Gamma_{\rm sep}`$ be the sum of their widths. String fragmentation generates the final states of

```math
\Gamma_{\rm frag} = \Gamma_{\rm had} - \Gamma_{\rm sep},
```

and exHad rejects string fragmentation events whose final state belongs to a separately generated
channel, so that no channel is counted twice.

A *channel group* $`F`$ is a set of hadronic final states whose total probability exHad fixes, for
example the group $`4\pi`$, which contains $`2(\pi^+\pi^-)`$ and $`\pi^+\pi^-2\pi^0`$. The probability of
group $`F`$ among the decays generated by string fragmentation is

```math
P_F = \frac{\Gamma_F}{\Gamma_{\rm frag}}, \qquad P_R = 1 - \sum_F P_F ,
```

where $`\Gamma_F`$ is the partial width into $`F`$ and $`P_R`$ is the probability of the *remainder* $`R`$,
all other allowed final states. The branching ratio into $`F`$ is
$`\mathrm{Br}_F = (\Gamma_{\rm frag}/\Gamma_{\rm tot})\,P_F`$. The groups of each model are listed in
its section.

exHad redistributes $`\Gamma_{\rm had}`$ among the hadronic channels and leaves $`\Gamma_{\rm had}`$
itself unchanged. Its value comes from outside the constrained fragmentation model.

Above the mass at which a model's total hadronic width becomes a perturbative estimate, every model
receives the same two kinds of external rate:

- **The total inclusive width**, divided into the width of a decay into each quark–antiquark pair and
  into a gluon pair, computed in perturbative QCD. Such a width says how often a decay of that kind
  happens and leaves open which hadrons it produces.
- **The widths of selected exclusive channels**, each a decay into a fixed set of hadrons, taken from
  measured cross sections or from analytic calculations. exHad generates these channels without string
  fragmentation and rejects fragmentation events that fall into them, so that no channel is counted
  twice.

The constrained fragmentation model turns the first kind into final states and holds the second kind
fixed. The mass at which the switch happens, and the sources of both kinds, differ by model:

- `dark-photon` and `b-l`: the perturbative QCD width of decays into light quarks (for
  `dark-photon` joined to the sum of the exclusive widths where the exclusive calculation ends), plus the width
  into charmed hadrons obtained from the measured cross section of $`e^+e^-`$ annihilation into
  hadrons;
- `alp-fermion` and the scalar models: the tabulated widths of the input calculations, which above
  the exclusive region consist of the widths into gluon pairs, quark–antiquark pairs and nucleon
  pairs;
- `hnl`: the tabulated widths of arXiv:1805.08567 into a lepton plus one meson or two pions and
  into a lepton plus a quark–antiquark pair. The two kinds sit side by side at every mass, with the
  switch made for each weak current, quark flavour combination and mixing flavour separately
  ([Decay channels and matching of exclusive and inclusive widths](#decay-channels-and-matching-of-exclusive-and-inclusive-widths)).

### Quantum numbers of the decaying particle

String fragmentation conserves charge, baryon number and flavour; exHad additionally rejects
hadronic states whose isospin, G parity, C parity or two-body spin and parity are incompatible with
the decaying particle.

The partons of a decay are produced by a current with definite quantum numbers. For currents that
mix several components, such as the electromagnetic current of `dark-photon`, exHad generates each
component separately with its own quantum numbers. The table lists the quantum numbers imposed on
each component.

| Particle and component of its current | $`J^P`$ | $`I`$, $`I_3`$ | $`G`$ | $`C`$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dark-photon`: $`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$, isovector vector current | $`1^-`$ | 1, 0 | $`+`$ | $`-`$ |
| `dark-photon` and `b-l`: $`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$, isoscalar vector current | $`1^-`$ | 0, 0 | $`-`$ | $`-`$ |
| `dark-photon` and `b-l`: $`s\bar s`$, vector current | $`1^-`$ | 0, 0 | $`-`$ | $`-`$ |
| `alp-fermion`: $`gg`$, $`s\bar s`$ and $`c\bar c`$ | $`0^-`$ | 0, 0 | $`+`$ | $`+`$ |
| Scalar models: $`gg`$ and $`s\bar s`$; in the parton shower description also $`c\bar c`$ and $`b\bar b`$ | $`0^+`$ | 0, 0 | $`+`$ | $`+`$ |
| `hnl`: $`u\bar d`$ charged current, vector part | $`1^-`$ | 1, $`\pm 1`$ | $`+`$ | — |
| `hnl`: $`u\bar d`$ charged current, axial vector part | $`1^+`$ | 1, $`\pm 1`$ | $`-`$ | — |
| `hnl`: $`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$ neutral current, isovector vector part | $`1^-`$ | 1, 0 | $`+`$ | — |
| `hnl`: $`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$ neutral current, isovector axial vector part | $`1^+`$ | 1, 0 | $`-`$ | — |
| `hnl`: $`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$ neutral current, isoscalar vector part | $`1^-`$ | 0, 0 | $`-`$ | — |
| `hnl`: $`s\bar s`$ neutral current, vector part | $`1^-`$ | 0, 0 | $`-`$ | — |
| `hnl`: $`s\bar s`$ neutral current, axial vector part | $`1^+`$ | 0, 0 | $`+`$ | — |
| `hnl`: $`c\bar d`$ charged current | — | 1/2, $`\pm 1/2`$ | — | — |
| `hnl`: $`c\bar s`$ charged current | — | 0, 0 | — | — |

A dash means that the quantity is not tested. For the charged currents, the sign of $`I_3`$ is the
sign of the electric charge of the hadronic system. The tests apply to the *primary hadrons*,
the hadrons formed in string fragmentation before any of them decays; weak decays can later change
strangeness and isospin. A generated state is

- rejected if the third components of the hadron isospins do not add up to $`I_3`$; otherwise it is
  kept with the probability that the hadron isospins couple to the listed total isospin $`I`$. This
  probability is computed by coupling the hadron isospins one at a time and summing the products of
  the squared Clebsch–Gordan coefficients over all intermediate isospins. Events of `alp-fermion`
  and of the scalar models generated by the parton shower description (see
  [Decays into quarks and gluons above 4 GeV](#decays-into-quarks-and-gluons-above-4-gev)) are
  rejected only when this probability vanishes;
- rejected if it consists only of mesons with integer isospin and definite G parity ($`\pi`$, $`\rho`$,
  $`\eta`$, $`\eta'`$, $`\omega`$, $`\phi`$) and the product of their G parities differs from $`G`$; states
  with kaons, baryons or other hadrons are not tested for G parity;
- rejected if it consists only of neutral mesons that are their own antiparticles, with known C
  parities, and the product of these C parities differs from $`C`$;
- rejected if it consists of two hadrons and no orbital angular momentum $`L`$ and total spin $`S`$
  give the listed $`J`$ with parity $`P = P_1 P_2 (-1)^L`$; where $`C`$ is tested, a particle–antiparticle
  pair must also have $`C = (-1)^{L+S}`$ equal to the listed $`C`$.

These restrictions change the composition most strongly below about 5 GeV, where a decay produces
few hadrons. They remove forbidden final states; the relative probabilities of the allowed final
states are the subject of the next subsection.

### Mass dependence of the probabilities of channel groups

Above the mass at which exclusive calculations end, the probability of each multihadron channel
group falls as $`s^{-2} = W^{-4}`$ in the invariant mass $`W`$ of the complete hadronic system, with
$`s = W^2`$, corrected near threshold by a finite-mass factor.

For a decaying boson of mass $`m`$, $`W = m`$. For `hnl`, the hadrons share the energy of the decay
with a charged lepton or a neutrino, and $`W`$ is the invariant mass of the quark–antiquark pair (see
[Heavy neutral lepton (`hnl`)](#heavy-neutral-lepton-hnl)).

The mass dependence is motivated by the particle $`A'`$ of `dark-photon`, which couples to quarks through the
electromagnetic current like the virtual photon in $`e^+e^-`$ annihilation. To leading order in the
couplings, both processes produce a hadronic final state $`F`$ through the same matrix element of this
current, so the partial widths follow from measured cross sections:

```math
\frac{\Gamma(A' \to F)}{\Gamma(A' \to \mu^+\mu^-)} = \frac{\sigma(e^+e^- \to F)}{\sigma(e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-)} \quad \text{at } s = m_{A'}^2 . \qquad (1)
```

Here $`\Gamma`$ is a partial width, $`\sigma`$ is a cross section with the effects of photon vacuum
polarization removed, and $`m_{A'}`$ is the mass of $`A'`$. BESIII measured that the cross sections of
$`e^+e^- \to 2(\pi^+\pi^-)`$ and $`e^+e^- \to K^+K^-\pi^+\pi^-`$ fall as $`s^{-3.18}`$ and $`s^{-3.12}`$ for
$`\sqrt{s}`$ between 3.77 and 4.6 GeV ([arXiv:2109.12751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.12751)). Away
from resonances and flavour thresholds, the cross section of $`e^+e^-`$ annihilation into all hadrons
made of light quarks falls as $`s^{-1}`$. By Eq. (1), the fraction of hadronic `dark-photon` decays
into these multihadron final states therefore falls approximately as $`s^{-2}`$.

exHad applies this mass dependence to the channel groups of `b-l`, `alp-fermion`, the scalar
models, and the `hnl` decays through the $`u\bar d`$ charged current and the $`u\bar u`$, $`d\bar d`$
neutral current. At a reference mass $`m_\ast`$, given for each model below, exclusive calculations
or measurements fix $`b_F = P_F(m_\ast^2)`$. At larger masses,

```math
P_F(s) = \frac{u_F(s)}{\mathcal{N}(s)}, \qquad u_F(s) = b_F \left(\frac{m_\ast^2}{s}\right)^{p} \frac{T_F(s)}{T_F(m_\ast^2)}, \qquad \mathcal{N}(s) = \max\Big[1,\ \sum_{F'} u_{F'}(s)\Big], \qquad p = 2 . \qquad (2)
```

The unnormalized probability $`u_F`$ equals $`b_F`$ at the reference mass and falls as $`s^{-p}`$ with a
power $`p`$ common to all groups. The factor $`T_F(s)`$ describes how the limited energy available near
threshold suppresses states with many hadrons: it rises with $`s`$ and approaches a constant, so that
the $`s^{-p}`$ fall holds at larger masses. The normalization $`\mathcal{N}(s)`$ rescales all groups
together when their probabilities would add up to more than one. The remainder probability is
$`P_R = 1 - \sum_F P_F`$. For the electromagnetic current, Eqs. (1) and (2) give
$`\sigma(e^+e^-\to F) \propto s^{-(p+1)}`$ where $`T_F`$ is constant and $`\mathcal{N} = 1`$, so $`p = 2`$
corresponds to the measured $`s^{-3}`$.

The power $`p`$ is an empirical assumption over a finite mass range, tested against electromagnetic
data in the next subsection. The measured powers differ between channels: BESIII finds $`s^{-2.49}`$
for $`e^+e^- \to K^+K^-\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$, which corresponds to $`p \approx 1.5`$. The
[model variations](#model-variations) change $`p`$ and $`T_F`$.

exHad obtains $`T_F`$ from Pythia. Let $`Q_F(s)`$ be the Pythia probability of group $`F`$ for the
quark and gluon pairs of the model, after the quantum number restrictions and after removing the
separately generated channels, and let $`m_0`$ be a reference mass of this comparison with
$`s_0 = m_0^2`$. The extraction has three steps.

1. Fit $`Q_F(s) = A_F\, s^{-p_F}`$ for $`3 \le \sqrt{s} \le 5`$ GeV, with normalization $`A_F`$ and power
   $`p_F`$, and divide out this power:
   $`C_F(s) = [Q_F(s)/Q_F(s_0)]\,(s/s_0)^{p_F}`$. The ratio $`C_F`$ equals one at $`s_0`$, is constant for
   a pure power law, and approaches $`L_F = A_F/[Q_F(s_0)\, s_0^{p_F}]`$ at high mass.
2. Keep the rise only if the logarithmic fit has a coefficient of determination of at least 0.95,
   $`L_F > 1`$, $`\ln C_F`$ at the first mass above $`m_0`$ is positive by more than five standard
   deviations, and $`\lvert\ln(C_F/L_F)\rvert < 0.10`$ at every sampled mass from 3.5 GeV. Otherwise
   $`T_F = 1`$.
3. At the sampled masses $`s_i`$, in ascending order, take the largest value of $`C_F`$ reached so far,
   bounded by 1 and $`L_F`$:

```math
T_F(s_i) = \min\Big[L_F,\ \max\big(1,\ \max_{j \le i} C_F(s_j)\big)\Big] . \qquad (3)
```

$`T_F`$ therefore grows from one to $`L_F`$ and never decreases. Between the sampled masses, $`\ln T_F`$
is interpolated as a monotone cubic function of $`\ln s`$. Below 3 GeV only the shape of Pythia's mass
dependence enters; the level is fixed by $`b_F`$.

The mass $`m_0`$ sets the normalization of $`T_F`$, since $`T_F(m_0^2) = 1`$, and the exclusive
input at $`m_\ast`$ fixes $`P_F(m_\ast^2) = b_F`$. Where that input is one set of widths at a single
mass, that mass both fixes the level and starts the shape, so the two coincide:

| Application | Reference mass [GeV] |
|---|---|
| Test against electromagnetic data | 2.32 |
| `alp-fermion` | 1.911 |
| Scalar models | 2 |
| `b-l` | 2 for $`m_0`$, 3 for $`m_\ast`$ |
| `hnl`, $`u\bar d`$ charged current | 1.65 |
| `hnl`, $`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$ neutral current | 3 |

`b-l` is the exception, because its exclusive widths are calculated as functions of mass up to 3 GeV:
the level is fixed at the upper end of that calculation, and the shape enters from 2 GeV, the lowest
mass at which exHad uses it.

Every entry of the table is a value of $`W`$, so for the two `hnl` rows it is an invariant mass of the
quark–antiquark pair, which stays below the mass $`m_N`$ of the heavy neutral lepton by at least the
mass of the lepton produced with the pair. A bound on $`W`$ therefore leaves $`m_N`$ free above it,
and the matching masses of
[Decay channels and matching of exclusive and inclusive widths](#decay-channels-and-matching-of-exclusive-and-inclusive-widths)
are values of $`m_N`$.

The interval over which each model joins its exclusive probabilities to Eq. (2) is given in the
section of that model.

### Test against electromagnetic data

With $`m_\ast = 2.32`$ GeV and the finite-mass factors, Eq. (2) describes 389 of 397 measured
exclusive $`e^+e^-`$ cross sections within a factor of two.

The constrained fragmentation model is designed to predict the rates of exclusive channels within a
factor of two. The electromagnetic current is the one current for which many exclusive cross sections
are measured, so this comparison tests Eq. (2), which exHad applies to the other models.

For this test, the electromagnetic quark current is divided into its isovector part ($`u\bar u`$ and
$`d\bar d`$), its isoscalar light-quark part ($`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$) and its strange part
($`s\bar s`$). Their squared coefficients $`(Q_u - Q_d)^2/2`$, $`(Q_u + Q_d)^2/2`$ and $`Q_s^2`$, with the
quark charges $`Q_q`$ in units of the elementary charge, are in the ratio 9:1:2. The groups, their
final states and the high-mass values of $`T_F`$ are:

| Group | Final states | $`T_F`$ at high mass |
|---|---|---|
| $`3\pi`$ | $`\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$ | 1 |
| $`4\pi`$ | $`2(\pi^+\pi^-)`$, $`\pi^+\pi^-2\pi^0`$ | 1 |
| $`5\pi`$ | $`2(\pi^+\pi^-)\pi^0`$ | 1.38 |
| $`6\pi`$ | $`3(\pi^+\pi^-)`$, $`2(\pi^+\pi^-)2\pi^0`$ | 2.37 |
| $`\eta\pi\pi`$ | $`\eta\pi^+\pi^-`$ | 1 |
| $`\eta'\pi\pi`$ | $`\eta'\pi^+\pi^-`$ | 1 |
| $`K\bar K\pi`$ | $`K^+K^-\pi^0`$, $`K^+\bar K^0\pi^-`$ and its charge conjugate, $`K^0\bar K^0\pi^0`$ | 1 |
| $`K\bar K2\pi`$ | $`K^+K^-\pi^+\pi^-`$, $`K^+K^-2\pi^0`$ | 1.20 |
| $`K\bar K3\pi`$ | $`K^+K^-\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$ | 3.33 |
| $`4K`$ | $`2(K^+K^-)`$ | 3.36 |

Each group total is divided among its measured charge channels with their fractions at the
reference mass. A point agrees within a factor of two when the prediction divided by the measured
central value lies between 1/2 and 2. The comparison uses the BaBar and BESIII measurements listed
in the paper cited in the README, at masses below the threshold for producing a pair of charmed
mesons, excludes the charmonium windows at about 3.05–3.15 and 3.64–3.73 GeV, and excludes the
BaBar $`K^+K^-\pi^0`$ data with a cut on the kaon-pair mass. The points at 2.32 GeV, which fix
$`b_F`$, are not counted. The same measurements were used to develop the model, so the comparison
shows how closely the model reproduces them. With $`T_F = 1`$ for all groups, 331 of the 397
points agree within a factor of two; the six-pion points agree in 98 of 98 cases with $`T_F`$ and in
51 of 98 cases without it.

### Generation of hadronic events by rejection sampling

Each hadronic decay first chooses between the separately generated channels and fragmentation with
probabilities proportional to their widths, and fragmentation events are then accepted or rejected
so that each channel group occurs with probability $`P_F`$.

For string fragmentation, exHad chooses the partons of the decay and the component of the current,
together called a *source* $`a`$, with a probability $`r_a(s)`$ fixed by the couplings of the particle
and the widths of its rate calculation; for example, the current of `b-l` gives $`u\bar u`$ or
$`d\bar d`$ with probability 2/3 and $`s\bar s`$ with probability 1/3. The partons are placed back to
back in the rest frame of the decaying particle, and Pythia fragments them into a trial event.
Their direction is isotropic for `alp-fermion`, for the gluon and strange quark pairs of the scalar
models and for the `hnl` currents; for `dark-photon`, for `b-l` and for the charm quark pairs of the
scalar models, the density of the quark direction is proportional to $`1 + \cos^2\theta`$, where
$`\theta`$ is the angle to the $`z`$ axis of the returned event. exHad rejects the trial event if the quantum
numbers of the source forbid it or if its final state belongs to a separately generated channel.
The probability of group $`F`$ among the remaining trial events is

```math
Q_F(s) = \sum_a r_a(s)\, Q_{F,a}(s), \qquad \sum_a r_a(s) = 1,
```

where $`Q_{F,a}`$ is the probability of $`F`$ for source $`a`$, and $`Q_R = 1 - \sum_F Q_F`$. A trial event
in group $`F`$ receives the weight

```math
w_F(s) = \frac{P_F(s)}{Q_F(s)}, \qquad w_R(s) = \frac{P_R(s)}{Q_R(s)} . \qquad (4)
```

The weight raises the frequency of the groups that Pythia produces too rarely and lowers the
frequency of those it produces too often, so that the accepted events reproduce $`P_F`$.

Where measurements, an exclusive calculation or isospin symmetry give the probability $`f_{i|F}`$ of
the charge combination $`i`$ within group $`F`$, for example $`\eta\pi^+\pi^-`$ and $`\eta\pi^0\pi^0`$ within
$`\eta\pi\pi`$, the weight of an event in that combination is multiplied by $`f_{i|F}/q_{i|F}`$, where
$`q_{i|F}`$ is the probability of the combination among the trial events in $`F`$. This fixes the charge
fractions and leaves the probability of the group unchanged.

exHad accepts a trial event with probability $`w/M`$, where $`w`$ is its weight and $`M`$ the largest
weight at that mass. The accepted events belong to group $`F`$ with probability $`P_F`$, and within
each charge combination their momenta are those of Pythia's string fragmentation. The
probabilities $`Q_F`$ and $`q_{i|F}`$ were measured once in large Pythia samples at a grid of masses
and are distributed with exHad. Trial events use `StringFragmentation:stopMass = 0`, a parameter
that enters the mass at which Pythia closes a string into its last two hadrons; all other
fragmentation parameters keep the default values of Pythia8.317.

String fragmentation produces a few channel groups rarely or not at all: the quantum numbers of
`alp-fermion` forbid three pions, whose G parity is $`-1`$, and no set of primary hadrons gives
$`\pi^+\pi^-\gamma`$; the two-pion and $`K\bar K`$ groups of the `hnl` currents are produced too rarely
to sample. exHad generates the events of these groups directly with probability $`P_F`$, and the
model sections name them. For `dark-photon`, the channel groups with calculated or fitted rates
are separately generated channels, and string fragmentation generates only the remainder
([Dark photon (`dark-photon`)](#dark-photon-dark-photon)); the `hnl` descriptions at low $`W`$ are given
in [Hadrons from the quark–antiquark pair at invariant mass W](#hadrons-from-the-quarkantiquark-pair-at-invariant-mass-w).

In separately generated channels, the particles produced directly in the decay, which can be
resonances such as the $`\omega`$ in $`\omega\pi^+\pi^-`$, have two-body momenta distributed isotropically
and multibody momenta distributed over phase space; where a model section gives a different
distribution, it states it. Pythia then decays the unstable particles.

[SAMPLING.md](SAMPLING.md) gives the exact algorithm and the accelerated sampler that exHad uses by
default for some models. `alp-fermion` samples can also be generated with event weights
([INTERFACES.md](INTERFACES.md#weighted-alp-fermion-samples)).

### Decays into quarks and gluons above 4 GeV

From 5 GeV exHad generates the hadrons with Pythia's final-state parton shower followed by string
fragmentation, and between 4 and 5 GeV each event is taken as a whole from one of the two
descriptions with a smoothly rising probability.

At higher masses, the partons of the decay radiate further gluons and quark–antiquark pairs before
they form hadrons. In a decay into a gluon pair or a quark–antiquark pair, for example $`S\to gg`$ or
$`S\to b\bar b`$, exHad places the two partons back to back in their rest frame with invariant mass
$`W`$ and an isotropic direction. Pythia simulates the emission of gluons and quark–antiquark pairs
by these outgoing partons (the final-state parton shower), once per event and starting from the
scale $`W/2`$, and then applies string fragmentation, both with Pythia's default parameters. This is
called the *parton shower description* below. It is used for `alp-fermion`, the
scalar models, and the `hnl` decays into a lepton and a quark–antiquark pair. `dark-photon` and
`b-l` use the constrained fragmentation model throughout their mass ranges.

Between $`W = 4`$ and 5 GeV the two descriptions are joined smoothly. Each event is taken from the
parton shower description with probability $`h(x)`$ and from the description used below 4 GeV with
probability $`1 - h(x)`$, where

```math
h(x) = 10x^3 - 15x^4 + 6x^5, \qquad x = \frac{W - 4~\mathrm{GeV}}{1~\mathrm{GeV}}, \qquad 0 \le x \le 1 . \qquad (5)
```

The polynomial $`h`$ rises from 0 at 4 GeV to 1 at 5 GeV, and its first and second derivatives vanish
at both ends, so channel fractions and momentum spectra change continuously and without kinks
across the interval. Momenta of different events are never averaged, and the partial widths of the
rate calculation stay unchanged. Below 4 GeV $`h = 0`$, and from 5 GeV all events come from the
parton shower description. The interval and the form of $`h`$ are a modelling choice.

The model-specific facts are:

- `alp-fermion`: the parton shower description starts from gluon pairs, $`s\bar s`$ pairs and
  $`c\bar c`$ pairs with
  the relative widths of the input calculation; nucleon pairs remain separately generated channels.
  exHad rejects its events whose primary hadrons cannot have $`J^{PC} = 0^{-+}`$, isospin zero and
  positive G parity. In particular, $`D\bar D`$ is forbidden, while $`D\bar D^\ast`$ with its charge
  conjugate, $`D^\ast\bar D^\ast`$ and states with more hadrons are allowed. The quarks have the
  masses 0.104 GeV ($`s`$) and 1.27 GeV ($`c`$).
- Scalar models: exHad rejects events of the parton shower description whose primary hadrons cannot
  have $`J^{PC} = 0^{++}`$,
  isospin zero and positive G parity, which allows $`D\bar D`$. The quarks have Pythia's constituent
  masses. The partons and the mass intervals are given in
  [Hadronic final states from 2 to 63 GeV](#hadronic-final-states-from-2-to-63-gev).
- `hnl`: the transition acts on the invariant mass $`W`$ of each quark–antiquark pair, event by event,
  for the currents with a description below 4 GeV; the pair keeps its flavour and four-momentum.
  The events of the parton shower description are accepted without a quantum number test. The
  descriptions of the individual currents are given in
  [Hadrons from the quark–antiquark pair at invariant mass W](#hadrons-from-the-quarkantiquark-pair-at-invariant-mass-w).

A shower or fragmentation that fails, and for `alp-fermion` and the scalar models a forbidden state,
is regenerated from partons with the same flavour and momenta, so the rejection does not change the
branching ratios. A request fails when the rate table gives a pair a positive rate below twice the
mass of the lightest hadron with its flavour ($`D^0`$ for $`c`$, $`B^+`$ for $`b`$). exHad then decays the
hadrons of the accepted event at full hadron level, so that the partonic decay channels of charm
and bottom hadrons fragment and conserve charge and baryon number.

### Exclusive decays below the masses of string fragmentation

Below the mass at which string fragmentation starts to generate the hadronic final states, every
hadronic decay is one of the exclusive channels of the input tables.

String fragmentation generates the hadronic final states from 1.70 GeV for `dark-photon`, from
1.911 GeV for `alp-fermion` and from 2.00 GeV for `b-l` and the scalar models. Below these masses
the tabulated exclusive widths describe the hadronic decays completely. exHad draws one channel
with probability proportional to its width among the channels that are open at the requested mass
and generates its final state directly: a two-body final state exactly, a three-body final state by
accepting or rejecting flat phase space with the squared matrix element of the input table, and a
final state of four or more particles with flat phase space. The $`\omega`$ and $`\phi`$ mesons of such
a channel are produced at their pole masses. The unstable hadrons then decay as in every other
event (see below).

The rate of an exclusive channel is zero at and below the sum of the masses of its particles and
rises linearly from zero there to the first tabulated mass above it. exHad draws a channel with a
probability equal to its rate divided by the sum of the rates of the open channels. Closing a
channel below its threshold therefore raises the probabilities of the open channels in proportion to
their rates and leaves their partial widths unchanged. This treatment also removes the widths that
the scalar input tables give the channels $`K^0_LK^0_L`$ and $`K^0_SK^0_S`$ between 0.988 and
0.995 GeV, below the threshold $`2m_{K^0} = 0.99522`$ GeV, because their tabulated masses follow the
charged-kaon threshold; the tables themselves are unchanged.

At these masses exHad switches directly between the two descriptions, with no interpolation
interval: below, one exclusive channel with phase space momenta and pole masses of the $`\omega`$
and $`\phi`$; above, a fragmented state whose resonances have their line shapes. Across 1.70 GeV the
hadronic branching fraction of `dark-photon` steps by 0.11%, the difference between the sum of the
exclusive widths and the fitted inclusive width of the electromagnetic current. The [model variations](#model-variations) change
the mass dependence of Eq. (2) and therefore leave the exclusive decays unchanged.

`hnl` has no such start mass: its tables list the exclusive channels and the decays into a lepton
and a quark–antiquark pair side by side at every mass, with the matching described in
[Decay channels and matching of exclusive and inclusive widths](#decay-channels-and-matching-of-exclusive-and-inclusive-widths),
and the same threshold rule applies to every channel of every model, including the decay of
`alp-fermion` into $`\tau^+\tau^-`$ and the channels with a $`b`$ quark of `hnl`, whose quark-level
thresholds close them below the lightest pair of hadrons that contains their quarks.

### Decays of unstable hadrons and τ leptons

The τ leptons and unstable hadrons produced in a decay are decayed, by Pythia except for some neutral
pion and $`K^0_S`$ decays, with the momenta of the primary decay products kept, so that only particles
needing detector transport remain.

Pythia decays these particles at full hadron level: a hadron decay into partons, as in some decays of
charm and bottom hadrons, is followed by string fragmentation of these partons, and charge and
baryon number are conserved. Decays of unstable particles include semileptonic decays and produce
neutrinos. The particles returned undecayed are listed in
[INTERFACES.md](INTERFACES.md#particles-left-for-detector-transport).

Neutral pions and $`K^0_S`$ mesons decay into two channels each, and exHad generates these decays in
two ways. In the events of the constrained fragmentation model and of its separately generated
channels, in the events generated from a charm quark pair of `dark-photon`, `b-l`, `alp-fermion`
and the scalar models, and in the hadrons that replace an `hnl` quark–antiquark pair below 4 GeV,
exHad decays $`\pi^0 \to \gamma\gamma`$, and $`K^0_S \to \pi^+\pi^-`$ with probability 0.692 and
$`K^0_S \to \pi^0\pi^0`$ otherwise, each isotropic in the rest frame of the decaying meson. In events
from the parton shower description, in the nonhadronic channels of samples of all decay channels,
and in the mesons produced directly in the `hnl` decays into one meson or two pions, Pythia decays
both mesons with its own tables, which add the Dalitz decay $`\pi^0 \to e^+e^-\gamma`$ and the rare
semileptonic $`K^0_S`$ decays.

## Nonhadronic decays and samples of all decay channels

When all decay channels are requested, exHad chooses each decay among the hadronic and nonhadronic
channels of the particle with probabilities equal to their branching ratios; this section gives
these channels, their widths and the momentum distributions of the primary decay products.

### Choice of the channel and momenta of the primary decay products

The channel is drawn from the branching ratios at the requested mass, and its primary decay products
are generated in the rest frame of the decaying particle.

The option that selects these samples is described in
[INTERFACES.md](INTERFACES.md#choice-of-decay-channels). For `dark-photon`, `b-l`, `alp-fermion`
and the scalar models, exHad first draws between the hadronic decays, with their total branching
ratio, and the individual nonhadronic channels. For `hnl`, it draws one of the individual hadronic
and nonhadronic channels listed in
[Decay channels and matching of exclusive and inclusive widths](#decay-channels-and-matching-of-exclusive-and-inclusive-widths).
The models contain no decays into additional invisible new particles.

The nonhadronic decays into two particles are isotropic. Decays into three particles, which occur for `hnl`, are
distributed over the Dalitz plot with the density of phase space times the squared matrix element
of arXiv:1805.08567, sampled by exact accept–reject; where the tabulated squared matrix element is
negative, the density is set to zero. Colourless unstable primary particles, such as τ leptons or
the $`\omega`$ in $`N \to \omega\nu`$, are then decayed by Pythia with their momenta held fixed.

In a `hnl` decay into a lepton and a quark–antiquark pair, the momenta of the lepton and of the pair
follow the three-body matrix element, with $`W`$ restricted to values above the sum of the approximate
masses of the lightest mesons that contain the two quarks (for example $`2m_\pi`$ for $`u\bar d`$).
exHad then replaces the pair by hadrons at this $`W`$ (see
[Hadrons from the quark–antiquark pair at invariant mass W](#hadrons-from-the-quarkantiquark-pair-at-invariant-mass-w)),
and a τ lepton produced with the pair is decayed afterwards.

## Dark photon (`dark-photon`)

The particle $`A'`$ of `dark-photon` couples to the electromagnetic current, so its exclusive hadronic widths follow from
measured $`e^+e^-`$ cross sections through Eq. (1).

### Interaction

The dark photon $`A'`$ couples to the electromagnetic current with strength $`\epsilon e`$.

```math
\mathcal{L} \supset -\epsilon\, e\, A'_\mu J^\mu_{\rm EM}, \qquad J^\mu_{\rm EM} = \sum_f Q_f\, \bar f\gamma^\mu f,
```

where $`\epsilon`$ is the dimensionless kinetic mixing parameter, $`e`$ the elementary charge and $`Q_f`$
the electric charge of fermion $`f`$ in units of $`e`$. The lifetime table that `model_info` returns
for `dark-photon` gives the decay length $`c\tau`$ in metres for $`\epsilon = 1`$; for another value of
$`\epsilon`$, the decay length is $`c\tau/\epsilon^2`$.

### Hadronic decay rates from electromagnetic data and exclusive calculations

Below 2 GeV exclusive calculations give the channel widths, above 2.5 GeV fits to measured cross
sections give the rates of the main multipion and $`K\bar K\pi\pi`$ channels, and a smooth
interpolation joins them.

Let $`\Gamma_{uds}`$ be the partial width into hadrons made only of $`u`$, $`d`$ and $`s`$ quarks. In
perturbative QCD, $`\Gamma_{uds} = R_{uds}(m)\,\Gamma(A'\to\mu^+\mu^-)`$, where $`R_{uds}`$ is the
contribution of $`u`$, $`d`$ and $`s`$ quarks to $`R = \sigma(e^+e^-\to\text{hadrons})/\sigma(e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-)`$,
calculated with QCD corrections to fourth order in the strong coupling as in
[arXiv:2201.01788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01788). At 1.70 GeV the sum of the exclusive widths
of that calculation crosses the perturbative width. Between 1.70 and 2.00 GeV, exHad multiplies the
perturbative width by $`(1-h)\,r(m) + h`$, where $`r(m)`$ is the ratio of the sum of the exclusive widths
to the perturbative width and $`h`$ is the polynomial of Eq. (5) with
$`x = (m - 1.70~\mathrm{GeV})/(0.30~\mathrm{GeV})`$. $`\Gamma_{uds}`$ therefore changes smoothly from the
exclusive sum at 1.70 GeV to its perturbative value, which it keeps above 2.00 GeV.

The final states of $`\Gamma_{uds}`$ are generated as follows.

- *Separately generated channels at all masses*: $`\pi^+\pi^-`$, $`\pi^0\gamma`$, $`\eta\gamma`$,
  $`K\bar K`$ ($`K^+K^-`$ and $`K^0_L K^0_S`$), $`K\bar K\pi`$, $`p\bar p`$ and $`n\bar n`$, with the form factors
  of [arXiv:1801.04847](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04847) and arXiv:2201.01788. The $`K\bar K\pi`$ widths
  of the form factor calculation end at 4 GeV; above, each of its contributions falls as $`m^{-3}`$
  times an exponential factor chosen so that the value and the slope match at 4 GeV.
- *Between 1.70 and 2.00 GeV*, the exclusive calculation gives the fractions $`\Gamma_F/\Gamma_{uds}`$ of its
  channels (for $`p\bar p`$ and $`n\bar n`$ directly their widths). They include the separately generated
  channels and
  $`\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$, $`2(\pi^+\pi^-)`$, $`\pi^+\pi^-2\pi^0`$, $`\eta\pi^+\pi^-`$, $`\eta'\pi^+\pi^-`$,
  $`\eta\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$, $`\eta K\bar K`$, $`K^+K^-\pi^+\pi^-`$ and the $`K\bar K\pi\pi`$ states with a
  neutral pion or kaon, the five- and six-pion final states, the measured final states that contain
  a photon, such as $`\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0\gamma`$ and $`\pi^0\pi^0\gamma`$, and $`\pi^0\pi^0K^0_LK^0_S`$.
- *Above 2.00 GeV*, the fractions $`\Gamma_F/\Gamma_{uds}`$ of seven final states follow fits to BaBar
  and BESIII cross sections: $`\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$, $`2(\pi^+\pi^-)`$, $`\pi^+\pi^-2\pi^0`$, $`3(\pi^+\pi^-)`$,
  $`2(\pi^+\pi^-)2\pi^0`$, $`K^+K^-\pi^+\pi^-`$, and the $`K\bar K\pi\pi`$ states that contain a neutral pion
  or kaon. Each fitted cross section is the squared modulus of a sum of Breit–Wigner amplitudes with
  fixed masses and constant widths and one smooth continuum amplitude, with complex coefficients
  fitted to the data. The fraction is the fitted
  cross section divided by $`R_{uds}\,\sigma(e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-)`$. Between 2.0 and 2.5 GeV, each
  fraction is a cubic polynomial in the mass that reproduces the value and the mass derivative of the
  exclusive calculation at 2.0 GeV and of the fit at 2.5 GeV; above 2.5 GeV the fits give the
  fractions directly.
- *The remainder*, the part of $`\Gamma_{uds}`$ not assigned to the channels above, is generated by
  string fragmentation, separately for the isovector, isoscalar light-quark and strange parts of the
  current with relative probabilities 9:1:2. exHad rejects states forbidden by the quantum numbers of
  each part (see [Quantum numbers of the decaying particle](#quantum-numbers-of-the-decaying-particle))
  and states in the channels above. Above 2 GeV, channels outside the seven fits and the separately
  generated channels, for example $`\eta\pi^+\pi^-`$, belong to the remainder.

*Momenta in the channels with calculated or fitted widths.* exHad generates these final states from
the intermediate resonance states of the exclusive calculation, for example $`\omega\pi^0\pi^0`$ or
$`\phi\pi^0`$ in $`\pi^+\pi^-2\pi^0`$, with relative probabilities from that calculation. Above 2 GeV
these relative probabilities are held at their values at 2 GeV, except in $`K\bar K`$ and $`K\bar K\pi`$,
which follow their form factors. The momenta of the intermediate states are distributed over phase
space, with resonance masses drawn from Pythia's line shapes, and Pythia decays the resonances; exHad
regenerates the event until the decays produce the selected final state.

*Charge combinations of the remainder.* For an event of the remainder whose primary hadrons are all
pions, exHad draws the charge combination $`c = (n_+, n_0, n_-)`$ of its $`n`$ pions from

```math
P_{\rm iso}(c \mid n, I, I_3) = \frac{\mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_c\, \Pi_{I,I_3})}{\sum_{c' \in \mathcal{A}} \mathrm{Tr}(\Pi_{c'}\, \Pi_{I,I_3})}, \qquad c \in \mathcal{A} . \qquad (10)
```

Here $`\Pi_c`$ projects on all orderings of pion charges with counts $`c`$, $`\Pi_{I,I_3}`$ on the states of
$`n`$ pions with the total isospin $`I`$ and third component $`I_3`$ of the current part, and
$`\mathcal{A}`$ contains the charge combinations that are kinematically open and do not belong to a
channel with a calculated or fitted width. Eq. (10) gives equal weight to every independent isospin
state of the pions compatible with the current, an assumption made in the absence of further
information on the amplitudes; it is applied only to pion multiplicities allowed by the G parity of
the current part. exHad regenerates the Pythia event until the drawn combination appears; after
65,536 unsuccessful attempts, the pion momenta of that combination are distributed over phase space.
Between 4 and 5 GeV, this charge assignment is used for a fraction $`1 - 3x^2 + 2x^3`$ of the remainder
events, with $`x = (m - 4~\mathrm{GeV})/(1~\mathrm{GeV})`$, and Pythia's charge combinations for the
others. This cubic polynomial falls from 1 at 4 GeV to 0 at 5 GeV with vanishing first derivatives
at both ends, so the charge fractions change continuously; all widths stay unchanged.

### Open charm

Above the $`D\bar D`$ threshold, the charm part of the measured ratio
$`R = \sigma(e^+e^-\to\text{hadrons})/\sigma(e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-)`$ gives the width into charmed hadrons.

The total hadronic width is $`\Gamma_{\rm had} = \Gamma_{uds} + \Gamma_c`$, where $`\Gamma_c`$ is the
partial width into final states with open charm, hadrons that contain a charm quark or antiquark. A
hadronic decay produces open charm with probability $`\Gamma_c/\Gamma_{\rm had}`$. For `dark-photon`,

```math
\Gamma_c = R_c(m)\,\Gamma(A'\to\mu^+\mu^-), \qquad R_c = \max\big(0,\ R_{\rm meas} - R_{uds}\big),
```

where $`R_{\rm meas}`$ is the value of $`R`$ measured by BES between 3.7 and 4.8 GeV
([hep-ex/0102003](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ex/0102003)) and at 5.0 GeV
([hep-ex/9908046](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ex/9908046)), as compiled by the Particle Data Group and
interpolated linearly in mass, and $`R_{uds}`$ is the perturbative value defined above. $`R_c`$ is zero
below the $`D^0\bar D^0`$ threshold at 3.73 GeV.

Measured exclusive cross sections divide $`\Gamma_c`$ among the final states $`D\bar D`$
($`D^0\bar D^0`$ and $`D^+D^-`$; [arXiv:0710.1371](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.1371)), $`D^{\ast+}D^-`$ with its
charge conjugate and $`D^{\ast+}D^{\ast-}`$ ([arXiv:2112.06477](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.06477)),
$`D_s^{\ast+}D_s^{\ast-}`$ ([arXiv:2305.10789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10789)),
$`\Lambda_c^+\bar\Lambda_c^-`$ ([arXiv:2307.07316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.07316)) and
$`\pi^+\pi^-D^+D^-`$ ([arXiv:2208.00099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.00099)). Each cross section is
interpolated linearly within its measured mass range and set to zero outside it; if their sum
exceeds the charm cross section $`R_c\,\sigma(e^+e^-\to\mu^+\mu^-)`$, all of them are reduced by a
common factor. $`D\bar D`$ is divided between $`D^0\bar D^0`$ and $`D^+D^-`$ in proportion to $`k^3`$, where
$`k`$ is the meson momentum in the decay: a vector current produces two pseudoscalar mesons in a P
wave. The charmed hadrons of these final states are produced at their pole masses with momenta
distributed over phase space, and Pythia decays them, including semileptonic decays. The rest of
$`\Gamma_c`$ is generated by Pythia string fragmentation of a $`c\bar c`$ pair.

A `dark-photon` charm event is regenerated when its final state, after the charm hadrons have
decayed, coincides with a light-hadron final state of a separately generated channel or of the seven
fits, so that no final state receives width from both descriptions. Charm events with leptons from
semileptonic decays never coincide with these final states.

### Accuracy and limitations

Measured cross sections constrain the exclusive rates of `dark-photon` directly.

- The eigenvector variations ([Model variations](#model-variations)) describe the uncertainties of the
  fitted coefficients of the seven cross sections. The uncertainties of the exclusive calculations,
  of the separately generated channels and of the charm rates are not varied.
- The remainder, whose composition string fragmentation determines, is about 40% of the hadronic
  decays at 2 GeV and 75% at 3 GeV.
- The narrow charmonium resonances $`J/\psi`$ (3.097 GeV) and $`\psi(2S)`$ (3.686 GeV) are not described;
  near these masses, the rates and the composition of the hadronic decays lack the resonance
  contributions.

## B−L gauge boson (`b-l`)

The gauge boson $`Z'`$ of `b-l` couples to the difference of baryon and lepton number, equally to $`u`$, $`d`$ and $`s`$
quarks, so its current has no isovector component.

### Interaction

The gauge boson $`Z'`$ couples to every fermion with its B−L charge, 1/3 for quarks and −1 for leptons and
neutrinos.

```math
\mathcal{L} \supset -g_{B-L}\, Z'_\mu J^\mu_{B-L}, \qquad J^\mu_{B-L} = \frac{1}{3}\sum_q \bar q\gamma^\mu q - \sum_{\ell = e,\mu,\tau}\left(\bar\ell\gamma^\mu\ell + \bar\nu_\ell\gamma^\mu P_L\nu_\ell\right),
```

where $`g_{B-L}`$ is the gauge coupling, $`q`$ runs over all quark flavours, and $`P_L = (1-\gamma_5)/2`$
selects left-handed neutrinos. The model has no kinetic mixing with the photon, and no right-handed
neutrinos are light enough to be produced in the decay.

### Light hadron rates

The exclusive light hadron widths come from vector-meson form factors rescaled to the B−L quark
couplings, and they are joined to Eq. (2) between 2 and 3 GeV.

The light quark current $`(\bar u\gamma^\mu u + \bar d\gamma^\mu d + \bar s\gamma^\mu s)/3`$ has
isospin zero. The decay into $`\pi^+\pi^-`$, which requires isospin one, is therefore absent in the
isospin limit, while $`\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$ is allowed. The exclusive widths use the decomposition by quark
flavour of the electromagnetic form factors fitted to $`e^+e^-`$ data
([arXiv:1801.04847](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04847), [arXiv:1911.11147](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.11147),
[arXiv:2201.01788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.01788)). Relative to the electromagnetic current, after
factoring out the couplings, the isovector amplitude vanishes, the isoscalar light-quark amplitude is
multiplied by two and the strange amplitude changes sign; the amplitudes are combined before the
widths are calculated.

The partial width into hadrons made of $`u`$, $`d`$ and $`s`$ quarks is the perturbative width

```math
\Gamma_{uds}(m) = \frac{g_{B-L}^2\, m}{12\pi}\,\big[1 + \delta_{\rm QCD}(m)\big], \qquad (11)
```

where $`m`$ is the mass of $`Z'`$, $`N_c\sum_{q=u,d,s}(1/3)^2 = 1`$ with $`N_c = 3`$ quark colours, and
$`\delta_{\rm QCD}`$ contains the QCD corrections to fourth order in the strong coupling of
arXiv:2201.01788.

The separately generated channels are $`\pi^0\gamma`$, $`\eta\gamma`$, $`K^+K^-`$, $`K^0_L K^0_S`$, $`p\bar p`$
and $`n\bar n`$, with their form factors evaluated at the requested mass. Let
$`G(m) = \Gamma_{uds}(m) - \Gamma_{\rm sep}(m)`$ be the width left for string fragmentation. The channel
groups and the high-mass values of their $`T_F`$ are:

| Group | Exclusive contributions | $`T_F`$ at high mass |
|---|---|---|
| $`3\pi`$ | $`\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$ | 1 |
| $`K\bar K\pi`$ | $`K\bar K\pi`$ | 1 |
| $`5\pi`$ | $`\omega\pi\pi`$ and $`\phi\pi\pi`$ with $`\omega, \phi \to \pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$ | 1.64 |
| $`\eta 3\pi`$ | $`\eta\omega`$ and $`\eta\phi`$ with $`\omega, \phi \to \pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$ | 1.10 |
| $`\eta K\bar K`$ | $`\eta\phi`$ with $`\phi \to K\bar K`$ | 1.74 |
| $`K\bar K\pi\pi`$ | $`K^\ast\bar K\pi`$, and $`\phi\pi\pi`$ with $`\phi \to K\bar K`$ | 2.54 |

The $`\omega`$ and $`\phi`$ decay branching ratios are fixed (0.892 for $`\omega\to 3\pi`$; 0.491, 0.340 and
0.154 for $`\phi\to K^+K^-`$, $`K^0_L K^0_S`$ and $`3\pi`$); the other $`\omega`$ and $`\phi`$ decays contribute
to the remainder.

Let $`E_F(m)`$ be the exclusive width of group $`F`$, calculated with the form factors up to 3 GeV. The
$`\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$ width near 2 GeV comes from a fit to BaBar
([arXiv:2110.00520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00520)) and BESIII
([arXiv:2401.14711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14711)) measurements of $`e^+e^-\to\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$
between 1.8 and 2.2 GeV, with $`\log\sigma`$ quadratic in $`\log(m/2~\mathrm{GeV})`$, converted to the
B−L current with the ratio of the form factor predictions for the two currents. Between 2.0 and
2.2 GeV this width joins the form factor prediction normalized to it at 2 GeV. The $`K\bar K\pi`$ width
uses the isoscalar normalization of the BaBar Dalitz plot analysis
([arXiv:0710.4451](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.4451)). Above 3 GeV, the group widths follow Eq. (2)
normalized at $`m_\ast = 3`$ GeV:

```math
H_F(m) = E_F(3~\mathrm{GeV})\,\frac{G(m)}{G(3~\mathrm{GeV})}\left(\frac{3~\mathrm{GeV}}{m}\right)^{2p}\frac{T_F(m^2)}{T_F(9~\mathrm{GeV}^2)},\qquad p = 2 . \qquad (12)
```

Dividing $`H_F`$ by $`G`$ gives $`P_F`$ of Eq. (2). Between 2 and 3 GeV, the partial width of each group is

```math
\Gamma_F(m) = \big[1 - h(x)\big]\, E_F(m) + h(x)\, H_F(m), \qquad x = \frac{m - 2~\mathrm{GeV}}{1~\mathrm{GeV}},
```

with $`h`$ of Eq. (5), so the widths follow the exclusive calculation at 2 GeV and Eq. (12) from 3 GeV
without kinks at either end. The remainder width is $`G - \sum_F\Gamma_F`$. Trial events come from
$`u\bar u`$ or $`d\bar d`$ strings of the isoscalar light-quark current with probability 2/3 and from
$`s\bar s`$ strings with probability 1/3, both with $`J^{PC} = 1^{--}`$, isospin zero and negative G parity.

### Open charm

Above the $`D\bar D`$ threshold a hadronic decay produces charmed hadrons with probability
$`\Gamma_c/(\Gamma_{uds} + \Gamma_c)`$, where $`\Gamma_c`$ is the electromagnetic charm width of the
[`dark-photon` open charm](#open-charm) multiplied by the squared-charge ratio $`(1/3)^2/(2/3)^2 = 1/4`$.

Explicitly,

```math
\Gamma_c = \frac{1}{4}\, R_c(m)\, \frac{g_{B-L}^2\, m}{12\pi}\left(1 + \frac{2m_\mu^2}{m^2}\right)\sqrt{1 - \frac{4m_\mu^2}{m^2}},
```

with $`R_c`$ and the muon mass $`m_\mu`$ as for `dark-photon`, and $`\Gamma_{\rm had} = \Gamma_{uds} + \Gamma_c`$.
The factor 1/4 compares the charm quark charge under the B−L current, 1/3, with its electric charge,
2/3. This assumes that the charm vector current produces all open charm; charm production by the light
quark currents and the interference between the light-quark and charm currents are neglected.

The charm final states are generated as for `dark-photon`: the measured exclusive final states with
their cross sections and a $`c\bar c`$ string for the rest of $`\Gamma_c`$. exHad identifies a charm
event before the weak decays of its charm hadrons and keeps it whatever final state these decays
produce.

### Accuracy and limitations

The light hadron rates of `b-l` follow from electromagnetic data and form factors through its
quark couplings.

- The transfer from the electromagnetic current relies on the flavour decomposition of the form
  factors; arXiv:1801.04847 estimates an uncertainty of about 20% from vector-meson dominance and
  flavour symmetry. The exclusive widths at 3 GeV are extrapolations of the form factors.
- About 68% of the width generated by string fragmentation at 2 GeV belongs to the remainder, whose
  composition string fragmentation determines.
- The charm width rests on the assumption that the charm vector current produces all open charm.
- A charm event is kept whatever final state the weak decays of its charm hadrons produce; in
  `dark-photon` such an event is generated again when its final state coincides with a separately
  generated or fitted channel.
- The narrow charmonium resonances $`J/\psi`$ and $`\psi(2S)`$ are not described (see the corresponding
  limitation of `dark-photon`).
- The [model variations](#model-variations) change the light hadron composition only; the
  uncertainties of the exclusive inputs and of the charm rates are not included.

## ALP coupled to fermions (`alp-fermion`)

The axion-like particle $`a`$ of `alp-fermion` couples with equal strength to all fermions, and its
hadronic widths come from the calculation of [arXiv:2501.04525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04525).

### Interaction

The particle $`a`$ couples derivatively to the axial currents of all fermions with a common
coefficient.

```math
\mathcal{L} \supset c_G\,\frac{\alpha_s}{4\pi}\,\frac{a}{f_a}\,G^a_{\mu\nu}\tilde G^{a\,\mu\nu} + \frac{\partial_\mu a}{f_a}\sum_F c_F\,\bar F\gamma^\mu\gamma_5 F,
```

where $`f_a`$ is the decay constant of $`a`$, an energy scale, $`F`$ runs over the Standard Model fermions, $`G^a_{\mu\nu}`$ is
the gluon field strength with dual $`\tilde G^{a\,\mu\nu}`$, and $`\alpha_s`$ is the strong coupling. The
coefficients $`c_F`$ and $`c_G`$ depend on the renormalization scale at which they are defined. The
model sets $`c_F = 1`$ for all fermions and $`c_G = 0`$ at the scale $`\Lambda = 1`$ TeV; the couplings at
the mass of $`a`$, including the effective coupling to gluons, follow from their renormalization group
evolution and loop contributions computed in arXiv:2501.04525. Axion-like particles with other
couplings at $`\Lambda`$, for example to gluons, photons or SU(2) gauge bosons, have different
exclusive rates and are not described by this model.

### Hadronic decay rates and their continuation to higher masses

The input calculation gives the exclusive hadronic widths and the widths into gluon, strange quark,
charm quark and nucleon pairs, and exHad joins its channel probabilities to Eq. (2) between 1.911 and
3 GeV.

The calculation includes the mixing of $`a`$ with pseudoscalar mesons. Parity forbids decays into two
pseudoscalar mesons, and near 1.9 GeV the decays into $`\eta\pi\pi`$ and $`\eta'\pi\pi`$ make up about 84%
and those into $`K\bar K\pi`$ about 13% of the width generated by string fragmentation. Above 1.911 GeV,
where the exclusive tables end, the tabulated hadronic width consists of the widths into gluon pairs,
$`s\bar s`$ pairs, $`c\bar c`$ pairs and nucleon pairs. String fragmentation starts from gluon pairs and
$`s\bar s`$ pairs with the relative probabilities of their widths (about 0.89 and 0.11 at 1.911 GeV).
The separately generated channels are $`p\bar p`$, $`n\bar n`$ and the decays assigned to $`c\bar c`$
pairs ([Open charm](#open-charm-2)).

The channel groups, their probabilities $`b_F`$ at $`m_\ast = 1.911`$ GeV and the high-mass values of
$`T_F`$ are:

| Group | Final states | $`b_F`$ | $`T_F`$ at high mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| $`\eta\pi\pi`$ | $`\eta\pi^+\pi^-`$, $`\eta\pi^0\pi^0`$, $`\eta'\pi^+\pi^-`$, $`\eta'\pi^0\pi^0`$ | 0.844 | 1.33 |
| $`K\bar K\pi`$ | a kaon pair and one pion | 0.126 | 1 |
| $`4\pi`$ | four pions | 0.015 | 5.83 |
| $`K\bar K\pi\pi`$ | kaons and two or more pions | 0.008 | 3.32 |
| $`5\pi`$ and more | five or more pions | 0.005 | 1 |
| $`3\pi`$ | $`\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$, $`3\pi^0`$ | 0.002 | 1 |
| $`\pi\pi\gamma`$ | $`\pi^+\pi^-\gamma`$ | 0.00006 | 1 |

At 1.911 GeV these groups exhaust the width generated by string fragmentation, so $`P_R = 0`$. exHad
generates the events of $`3\pi`$ and $`\pi\pi\gamma`$ directly with probability $`P_F`$: $`3\pi`$ as
$`\pi^+\pi^-\pi^0`$ (86%) or $`3\pi^0`$ (14%), the relative probabilities of the exclusive calculation at
1.911 GeV, with momenta distributed over phase space; $`\pi^+\pi^-\gamma`$ with the density of phase
space times $`\lvert\mathbf{p}_+\times\mathbf{p}_-\rvert^2`$, the momentum factor of its anomalous
amplitude with the pion momenta $`\mathbf{p}_\pm`$ in the rest frame of $`a`$ and a constant remaining
form factor.

Between 1.911 and 3 GeV, the vector $`\mathbf{P}`$ of all group probabilities $`P_F`$ and $`P_R`$ is
interpolated with a polynomial of fifth degree that reproduces the exclusive values $`\mathbf{b}`$ and
mass derivatives $`\mathbf{b}'`$ at $`m_\ast`$ and the values $`\mathbf{p}_1`$ and derivatives
$`\mathbf{p}_1'`$ of Eq. (2) at $`m_1 = 3`$ GeV. With $`L = m_1 - m_\ast`$ and $`x = (m - m_\ast)/L`$,

```math
\mathbf{P}(m) = \sum_{j=0}^{5}\binom{5}{j} x^j (1-x)^{5-j}\,\mathbf{c}_j , \qquad (13)
```

with $`\mathbf{c}_0 = \mathbf{b}`$, $`\mathbf{c}_1 = \mathbf{b} + L\mathbf{b}'/5`$,
$`\mathbf{c}_2 = (2\mathbf{c}_1 + \mathbf{c}_4)/3`$, $`\mathbf{c}_3 = (\mathbf{c}_1 + 2\mathbf{c}_4)/3`$,
$`\mathbf{c}_4 = \mathbf{p}_1 - L\mathbf{p}_1'/5`$ and $`\mathbf{c}_5 = \mathbf{p}_1`$. Every coefficient
vector is nonnegative and sums to one, so the probabilities are nonnegative and add up to one at every
mass. The shape within the interval is a modelling assumption. Above 3 GeV, Eq. (2) with $`b_F`$ at
1.911 GeV gives the probabilities. From 4 GeV, the join to the
[parton shower description](#decays-into-quarks-and-gluons-above-4-gev) applies.

### Charge combinations of ηππ and η′ππ

Because $`a`$ is an isospin singlet, the two pions in $`\eta\pi\pi`$ and $`\eta'\pi\pi`$ have isospin
zero, which in the equal-pion-mass limit gives charged and neutral combinations in the ratio 2:1.

```math
\frac{\Gamma(a\to\eta^{(\prime)}\pi^+\pi^-)}{\Gamma(a\to\eta^{(\prime)}\pi^0\pi^0)} = 2 . \qquad (14)
```

From 3 GeV, exHad imposes this ratio separately within $`\eta\pi\pi`$ and within $`\eta'\pi\pi`$, keeping
the probability of each of them; the division of the group between $`\eta\pi\pi`$ and $`\eta'\pi\pi`$
follows Pythia. Between 1.911 and 3 GeV, the probabilities of the four final states within the group
are interpolated with the fifth-degree polynomial of Eq. (13), from the values and slopes of the
exclusive calculation at 1.911 GeV, which include the small effects of the different charged and
neutral pion masses, to the values and slopes at 3 GeV given by Pythia's division with the ratio (14).
The eight charge combinations of $`K\bar K\pi`$ are interpolated in the same way from the exclusive
calculation at 1.911 GeV to Pythia's charge combinations at 3 GeV, which they follow above 3 GeV. These
charge probabilities enter the event weights as the factors $`f_{i|F}/q_{i|F}`$ of
[rejection sampling](#generation-of-hadronic-events-by-rejection-sampling); they change neither the
group probabilities nor any width, and within each charge combination the momenta follow Pythia.

### Open charm

Since $`a`$ cannot decay into two pseudoscalar mesons, $`D\bar D`$ is forbidden and open charm starts
at the $`D\bar D^\ast`$ threshold, so near that threshold part of the charm quark width produces light
hadrons.

The input calculation gives the width $`\Gamma_{c\bar c}`$ into charm quark pairs, which is nonzero from
about 3.74 GeV. A decay assigned to $`\Gamma_{c\bar c}`$ produces charmed hadrons with probability
$`\xi(m)`$ and light hadrons with probability $`1 - \xi(m)`$, where

```math
\xi(m) = \left[\frac{k(m)}{k(4~\mathrm{GeV})}\right]^3 \quad \text{for } m_{D^0} + m_{D^{\ast 0}} < m < 4~\mathrm{GeV}, \qquad (15)
```

$`\xi = 0`$ below this interval and $`\xi = 1`$ above it. Here $`k(m)`$ is the momentum of each meson in a
decay into $`D^0\bar D^{\ast 0}`$ of a particle of mass $`m`$ at rest. With Pythia's masses, the threshold
is at 3.87184 GeV. The third power is the threshold behaviour of the production of a pseudoscalar and
a vector meson in a P wave. The widths of the two outcomes add up to $`\Gamma_{c\bar c}`$ at every mass,
and the probabilities of the decays assigned to gluon pairs and $`s\bar s`$ pairs do not change.

- *Charmed hadrons*: Pythia string fragmentation of a $`c\bar c`$ pair, with the $`0^{-+}`$, isospin-zero
  restrictions on the primary hadrons, requiring a charm hadron and an anticharm hadron among them
  and rejecting $`D\bar D`$. Pythia decays the charm hadrons, including semileptonic decays.
- *Light hadrons*: Pythia string fragmentation of a gluon pair with the same restrictions, rejecting
  events with charm hadrons. These events keep Pythia's channel probabilities; within $`\eta\pi\pi`$ and
  $`\eta'\pi\pi`$, the charged and neutral combinations have probabilities 2/3 and 1/3.

The outcome is chosen once per decay and kept when the event is regenerated, so different generation
efficiencies of the two outcomes do not change their probabilities. Above 4 GeV all of
$`\Gamma_{c\bar c}`$ produces charmed hadrons; the events of the parton shower description in the
4–5 GeV interval obey the same restrictions
([Decays into quarks and gluons above 4 GeV](#decays-into-quarks-and-gluons-above-4-gev)).

### Accuracy and limitations

No measurement tests the channel probabilities of `alp-fermion`, so the model supports qualitative conclusions about
the hierarchy of hadronic channels and the resulting search signatures.

- The [model variations](#model-variations) do not assess the uncertainties of the exclusive
  calculation, including its division of $`K\bar K\pi`$ among charge combinations.
- The normalization of Eq. (15) at 4 GeV and the assignment of the rest of $`\Gamma_{c\bar c}`$ to light
  hadrons below 4 GeV are model assumptions. Decays into off-shell $`D\bar D\pi`$ and $`D\bar D\gamma`$
  states and narrow charmonium resonances are not described.

## Scalars mixing with the Higgs boson (`scalar-central`, `scalar-lower`, `scalar-upper`, `scalar-1809`)

The four scalar models describe one particle that mixes with the Higgs boson and differ only in the
calculation of its hadronic widths.

### Interaction

The scalar $`S`$ inherits the couplings of the Higgs boson suppressed by the mixing angle $`\theta`$.

```math
\mathcal{L} \supset -\sin\theta\,\frac{S}{v}\sum_f m_f\,\bar f f + \sin\theta\,\frac{\alpha_s}{12\pi v}\,S\,G^a_{\mu\nu}G^{a\,\mu\nu},
```

where $`m_f`$ is the fermion mass, $`v \approx 246`$ GeV the Higgs vacuum expectation value, and the second
term is the effective coupling to gluons through heavy quark loops, in the convention of
[arXiv:2407.13587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13587). The lifetime table that `model_info` returns for a
scalar model gives the decay length $`c\tau`$ in metres for $`\sin^2\theta = 1`$; for another mixing, the
decay length is $`c\tau/\sin^2\theta`$.

### Hadronic widths of the four input calculations

Each calculation gives the widths into $`\pi\pi`$ and $`K\bar K`$, the total width, and its gluon and
strange quark contributions; the four give the same widths into gluon pairs and $`s\bar s`$, $`c\bar c`$
and $`b\bar b`$ pairs. exHad uses them as follows.

| Model | Calculation of the hadronic widths |
|---|---|
| `scalar-central` | Dispersive calculation of the pion and kaon form factors in [arXiv:2407.13587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13587), anchored on measured data and carried out with its code [hipsofcobra](https://github.com/blackstonep/hipsofcobra): mean of its Monte Carlo sample, which propagates the uncertainties of the Omnès functions and of the low-energy matching coefficients |
| `scalar-lower` | The same calculation, one standard deviation below the mean |
| `scalar-upper` | The same calculation, one standard deviation above the mean |
| `scalar-1809` | Calculation of [arXiv:1809.01876](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01876), including the corrections and additional decay channels of [arXiv:1904.10447](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10447) |

The branching ratio tables of all four models also contain the decays into nucleon pairs, charged
leptons, gluon pairs and $`s\bar s`$, $`c\bar c`$ and $`b\bar b`$ pairs. Below 2 GeV, the light hadronic width
left after the decays into two mesons and nucleon pairs is assigned to four pions. exHad starts Eq. (2)
at $`m_\ast = 2`$ GeV with the single channel group $`4\pi`$ ($`2(\pi^+\pi^-)`$ and $`\pi^+\pi^-2\pi^0`$), so
$`b_{4\pi} = 1`$ and $`P_R = 0`$ at 2 GeV; Eq. (2) applies from 2 GeV without an interpolation interval. The
value 2 GeV is a phenomenological choice of the boundary between the exclusive and string fragmentation
descriptions. $`T_F`$ of $`4\pi`$ rises to 3.96 at high mass. Trial events come from gluon pairs and $`s\bar s`$
pairs with the relative probabilities of their tabulated widths (about 0.77 and 0.23 at 2 GeV).

The decays into $`\pi^+\pi^-`$, $`\pi^0\pi^0`$, $`K^+K^-`$, $`K^0_S K^0_S`$, $`K^0_L K^0_L`$, $`p\bar p`$ and $`n\bar n`$
are separately generated channels. The two-meson widths of the calculations end at a mass $`m_{\rm end}`$
just below 2 GeV. Above it, exHad holds each decay amplitude at its value at $`m_{\rm end}`$ and includes the
change of the two-body phase space:

```math
\Gamma_{hh}(m) = \Gamma_{hh}(m_{\rm end})\,\frac{m_{\rm end}}{m}\,\frac{\beta_h(m)}{\beta_h(m_{\rm end})}, \qquad \beta_h(m) = \sqrt{1 - \frac{4m_h^2}{m^2}}, \qquad (16)
```

separately for each charge combination $`hh`$ of pions or kaons with mass $`m_h`$ and for each of the four
calculations. These widths are subtracted from the widths of the gluon pairs and $`s\bar s`$ pairs, so the
total hadronic, leptonic, nucleon-pair and charm widths keep the mass dependence of the calculation.

The four models therefore differ as follows. Below 2 GeV they differ in the widths into $`\pi\pi`$ and
$`K\bar K`$ and in the four-pion width, and therefore in the total width, in the lifetime and in every
branching ratio; at 0.995 GeV the four lifetimes span a factor of 8.8 and at 1 GeV a factor of 4.1.
From 2 GeV they have the same total width and the same tabulated widths into gluon pairs, nucleon
pairs and $`s\bar s`$, $`c\bar c`$ and $`b\bar b`$ pairs, while their two-meson widths of Eq. (16) still
differ and take different shares of that total from the gluon pairs and $`s\bar s`$ pairs. At
2 GeV the two-meson channels take between 10% of the hadronic width in `scalar-lower` and 71% in
`scalar-upper`, and at 3 GeV between 3% and 20%.

That difference is the uncertainty of the dispersive calculation, continued upward from the last
mass at which the input tables give a two-meson width, $`m_{\rm end} = 1.999`$ GeV. At that mass
the widths of `scalar-lower` and `scalar-upper` differ by a factor of 1.9 in $`\pi\pi`$ and by a
factor of 16 in $`K\bar K`$. The $`\pi\pi`$ width of `scalar-1809` falls below both, so the four
span a factor of 6.3 in that channel; its $`K\bar K`$ width falls between them. Above $`m_{\rm end}`$
Eq. (16) multiplies each model's amplitude by the same function of the mass, so the ratio of any two
models in a given channel stays exactly what it is at $`m_{\rm end}`$ at every mass up to 5 GeV; the
shares themselves shrink together. From 5 GeV, $`1 - h = 0`$ in Eq. (5), so the contribution that
contains these separately generated two-meson channels vanishes and all four models give the same
distributions.

A decay into two mesons produces more than two final particles when the mesons themselves decay, as
$`\pi^0`$ and $`K^0_S`$ do; $`\pi^+\pi^-`$, $`K^+K^-`$ and $`K^0_LK^0_L`$ stay two particles,
because these mesons are left undecayed for detector transport. Of the hadronic decays that
`scalar-upper` generates at 2 GeV, 52% end with exactly two particles.

The decays into $`c\bar c`$ pairs, from their threshold at 3.73 GeV, are generated below 4 GeV by Pythia
string fragmentation of a $`c\bar c`$ pair without the quantum number restrictions.

### Hadronic final states from 2 to 63 GeV

exHad generates scalar decays with the constrained fragmentation model up to 4 GeV, with the 4–5 GeV
mixture of [Decays into quarks and gluons above 4 GeV](#decays-into-quarks-and-gluons-above-4-gev),
and with the parton shower description from 5 to 63 GeV; the branching ratio and lifetime tables
cover 0.01–63 GeV.

- *2–4 GeV*: the constrained fragmentation model of the previous subsection, with separately generated
  two-meson and nucleon-pair channels and $`c\bar c`$ strings.
- *4–5 GeV*: each event comes from the parton shower description with probability $`h`$ of Eq. (5) and
  from the
  description used below 4 GeV otherwise. The decays into two mesons of Eq. (16) belong to the latter, so
  their branching ratios fall as $`1 - h`$ and vanish at 5 GeV; the widths of the tables are unchanged.
- *5–63 GeV*: the parton shower description from gluon pairs and $`s\bar s`$, $`c\bar c`$ and $`b\bar b`$
  pairs at the tabulated
  rates, with the separately generated decays into nucleon pairs.

The tabulated width into $`b\bar b`$ pairs is positive above 10.542 GeV, which lies below the $`B^+B^-`$
threshold at 10.5585 GeV; between these two masses no pair of bottom hadrons can form, and requests
fail with an error.

### Accuracy and limitations

No measurement tests the channel probabilities of the scalar models; the four models show the spread
between available calculations of the hadronic widths.

- The spread of the four models includes the correlated change of the total hadronic width. The
  continuation of the two-meson amplitudes by Eq. (16) above 2 GeV is an assumption whose uncertainty is
  not covered by this spread or by the [model variations](#model-variations).
- Below 4 GeV the decays into $`c\bar c`$ pairs are hadronized without the quantum number test, so they
  contain $`D\bar D^\ast`$ and its charge conjugate, which parity forbids for a spin-zero particle of
  positive parity and which the parton shower description rejects above 4 GeV.

## Heavy neutral lepton (`hnl`)

The heavy neutral lepton $`N`$ is a Majorana fermion that mixes with the three active neutrinos and decays
through the charged and neutral weak currents with rates computed as in
[arXiv:1805.08567](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08567).

### Interaction

The mixing $`U_\alpha`$ with the neutrino of flavour $`\alpha`$ gives $`N`$ the charged- and neutral-current
couplings of that neutrino, suppressed by $`U_\alpha`$.

```math
\mathcal{L} \supset \frac{g}{2\sqrt{2}}\,W^+_\mu\,\overline{N^c}\sum_\alpha U^\ast_\alpha\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma_5)\,\ell^-_\alpha + \frac{g}{4\cos\theta_W}\,Z_\mu\,\overline{N^c}\sum_\alpha U^\ast_\alpha\gamma^\mu(1-\gamma_5)\,\nu_\alpha + \mathrm{h.c.},
```

where $`\alpha = e, \mu, \tau`$, $`g`$ is the weak coupling, $`\theta_W`$ the weak mixing angle and $`N^c`$ the
charge conjugate field (Eq. (3) of arXiv:1805.08567). Since $`N`$ is a Majorana fermion, each decay channel
and its charge conjugate have equal widths. The width table that `model_info` returns for `hnl` lists
the mass $`m_N`$ and the total widths $`\Gamma_e`$, $`\Gamma_\mu`$ and $`\Gamma_\tau`$ in GeV for
$`\lvert U_\alpha\rvert^2 = 1`$; the total width for general mixings is
$`\Gamma_N = \sum_\alpha \lvert U_\alpha\rvert^2\,\Gamma_\alpha`$.

### Decay channels and matching of exclusive and inclusive widths

For each weak current, quark flavour combination and mixing flavour, exHad uses exclusive meson channels
below the mass at which the inclusive quark-level width exceeds their sum, and above it a lepton plus
quark–antiquark pair whose width is the inclusive width minus the widths of the one-meson channels.

The hadronic channels of `hnl` are:

- *Decays into one meson*: $`N \to \pi^\pm\ell^\mp`$, $`K^\pm\ell^\mp`$, $`D^\pm\ell^\mp`$ and
  $`D_s^\pm\ell^\mp`$ with a charged lepton $`\ell`$, and $`N \to \pi^0\nu`$, $`\eta\nu`$, $`\eta'\nu`$, $`\omega\nu`$,
  $`\phi\nu`$, $`a_1\nu`$ and $`\eta_c\nu`$ with a neutrino. In string fragmentation a final state with a single
  meson has zero phase space measure, so Pythia does not produce these decays; exHad generates them as
  separately generated channels at all masses.
- *Decays into two pions* $`N \to \pi^\pm\pi^0\ell^\mp`$ (charged current) and $`N \to \pi^+\pi^-\nu`$
  (neutral current), below the matching mass of the corresponding current and mixing.
- *Decays into a lepton and a quark–antiquark pair*, such as $`N \to \ell^- u\bar d`$ through the charged
  current and $`N \to \nu q\bar q`$ through the neutral current, and their charge conjugates.

The tables use matching between the exclusive and inclusive descriptions of decays via the charged and
neutral currents, which depends on the mixing. For each current, each combination of quark flavours and
each mixing flavour $`\alpha`$, the matching mass is the mass $`m_N`$ at which the inclusive width into a
lepton and a quark–antiquark pair, calculated with QCD corrections, exceeds the sum of the exclusive
hadronic widths. Below it, the exclusive channels describe the hadronic decays of that current. Above it,
the decays into one meson keep their widths, the sum of these widths is subtracted from the inclusive
width of the same current and mixing, and the difference is the width of the decay into a lepton and a
quark–antiquark pair; its multihadron final states replace the exclusive multihadron decays of lower
masses. There is no common matching mass. The table gives the intervals between two tabulated masses
$`m_N`$ in which the width into the lepton and the quark–antiquark pair becomes nonzero; for the currents
with exclusive channels, the matching mass lies in this interval. For the neutral current the three
mixings give the same interval, while the charged lepton masses separate those of the charged
currents.

| Quark–antiquark pair | Current | Electron mixing [GeV] | Muon mixing [GeV] | Tau mixing [GeV] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $`u\bar d`$ | charged | 1.845–1.850 | 1.860–1.865 | 3.61–3.64 |
| $`u\bar s`$ | charged | 1.375–1.380 | 1.395–1.400 | 2.89–2.92 |
| $`c\bar d`$ | charged | 3.13–3.16 | 3.16–3.19 | 4.54–4.57 |
| $`c\bar s`$ | charged | 3.52–3.55 | 3.55–3.58 | 4.87–4.90 |
| $`u\bar b`$ | charged | 5.41–5.44 | 5.50–5.53 | 7.18–7.21 |
| $`c\bar b`$ | charged | 7.12–7.15 | 7.24–7.27 | 8.92–8.95 |
| $`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$ | neutral | 1.375–1.380 | 1.375–1.380 | 1.375–1.380 |
| $`s\bar s`$ | neutral | 2.56–2.59 | 2.56–2.59 | 2.56–2.59 |
| $`c\bar c`$ | neutral | 5.77–5.80 | 5.77–5.80 | 5.77–5.80 |

exHad takes all widths from these tables and does not change them or the lifetime of $`N`$.

For given squared mixings, exHad selects the mixing flavour and the channel as described in
[Nonhadronic channels and their widths](#nonhadronic-channels-and-their-widths). A sample of the
decays of `hnl` that contain hadrons consists of its semileptonic decays
([INTERFACES.md](INTERFACES.md#choice-of-decay-channels)).

### Hadrons from the quark–antiquark pair at invariant mass W

exHad replaces the quark–antiquark pair by hadrons with the same total four-momentum, using a description
that depends on the quark flavours of the current and on $`W`$.

In the decays $`N \to \nu q\bar q`$ and $`N \to \ell^- q\bar q'`$, $`W`$ is the invariant mass of the
quark–antiquark pair; it is smaller than $`m_N`$ by at least the lepton mass. exHad generates the hadrons in
the rest frame of the pair and boosts them with its four-momentum; the momentum of the lepton or neutrino
stays unchanged. The hadrons of a charge conjugate decay are the charge conjugates of the hadrons
described below. The descriptions below 4 GeV are joined to the parton shower description between
$`W = 4`$ and 5 GeV
([Decays into quarks and gluons above 4 GeV](#decays-into-quarks-and-gluons-above-4-gev)).

The constrained fragmentation model generates the hadrons directly at the exact $`W`$. The other
descriptions below generate them at a nearby point of a grid in $`W`$ (mostly with 25 MeV spacing; 250 MeV
for the $`u\bar s`$ current above 1.65 GeV) and multiply all hadron momenta in the rest frame of the pair by
a common factor so that the total energy equals the exact $`W`$. A final state whose hadron masses add up
to more than the exact $`W`$ is generated again directly at that $`W`$.

#### u d̄ charged current

The channel fractions come from the hadronic mass spectra of nonstrange τ decays measured by ALEPH
([hep-ex/0506072](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ex/0506072)), with the updated spectral functions of
[arXiv:1312.1501](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.1501). Written for a hadronic system of charge −1, they
give, as functions of $`W`$, the fractions of $`\pi^-\pi^0`$, $`\pi^-2\pi^0`$, $`2\pi^-\pi^+`$, $`2\pi^-\pi^+\pi^0`$,
$`\pi^-3\pi^0`$, five pions, $`\eta\pi^-\pi^0`$, $`K^-K^0`$, $`K\bar K\pi`$ and other final states, and the
relative contributions of the vector and axial vector parts of the current.

- *W < 1 GeV*: exHad generates the final states directly from their primary hadrons with these
  fractions: $`\pi^-\pi^0`$; $`\pi^-2\pi^0`$ and $`2\pi^-\pi^+`$, as $`\rho\pi`$ above 0.95 GeV and with
  phase-space momenta below; $`\eta\pi^-\pi^0`$; and $`K^-K^0`$. The small four-pion and other fractions are
  added to three pions.
- *1 GeV ≤ W ≤ 1.65 GeV*: exHad generates $`\pi^-\pi^0`$, $`\pi^-3\pi^0`$ (as $`\rho^-\pi^0\pi^0`$, from
  1.046 GeV), $`K^-K^0`$,
  $`K\bar K\pi`$ (as $`K^\ast\bar K`$, from 1.40 GeV) and $`\eta\pi^-\pi^0`$ (as $`\eta\rho^-`$ above 1.324 GeV) directly with
  their measured fractions. A string fragmentation event whose final state belongs to one of these
  channels is rejected, so they keep exactly their measured fractions. The other events come from
  Pythia string fragmentation of the quark pair, with
  the quantum number restrictions of the two parts and with trial events
  from the vector part ($`J^P = 1^-`$, $`G = +`$) and the axial vector part ($`1^+`$, $`G = -`$) in proportion to
  their spectral functions. Ten fragmentation parameters of Pythia (`StringFragmentation:stopNewFlav`,
  `stopMass` and `stopSmear`, `StringFlav:probStoUD`, `mesonUDvector`, `mesonSvector` and `etaSup`,
  `StringZ:aLund` and `bLund`, `StringPT:sigma`) and a factor multiplying the axial vector contribution
  were fitted to the measured channel fractions at $`W`$ = 1.00, 1.10, 1.20, 1.30, 1.40, 1.50, 1.60 and
  1.65 GeV and are interpolated linearly in $`W`$. Each remaining string fragmentation event is accepted
  with a probability proportional to the ratio of the measured fraction of its final state to the
  fraction produced by the fitted string fragmentation (at most 15), so that these final states occur with
  their measured fractions.
- *1.650–1.651 GeV*: the channel fractions measured at 1.65 GeV, continued in $`W`$ with their value
  and slope, give the probability of each final state, and the final state is generated as at
  1.65 GeV.
- *1.651 GeV < W ≤ 5 GeV*: the constrained fragmentation model with $`m_\ast = 1.65`$ GeV, $`b_F`$ equal to the
  measured fractions at 1.65 GeV, and trial events from the vector and axial vector parts with the relative
  weights 0.54 and 0.46 of the spectral functions at 1.65 GeV. The channel groups are two pions, three
  pions, four pions, five or more pions, $`K\bar K`$, $`K\bar K\pi`$ and $`\eta\pi\pi`$; $`T_F`$ rises to 2.32 for
  four pions and 6.58 for five or more pions and equals 1 for the others. exHad generates the two-pion and
  $`K\bar K`$ groups directly with probability $`P_F`$, as isotropic two-body decays.

#### u ū and d d̄ neutral current

The isovector vector and axial vector parts of the neutral current follow from the τ spectral functions
by isospin symmetry, with the weak neutral-current couplings of the quarks, and the isoscalar vector part
from the electromagnetic form factors of [arXiv:1801.04847](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.04847). Together
they give the fractions of $`\pi^+\pi^-`$, three pions, four pions, and five pions with all other final
states, as functions of $`W`$.

- *W < 1 GeV*: exHad generates $`\pi^+\pi^-`$ and three pions (as $`\rho\pi`$ above 0.95 GeV, with
  phase-space momenta below) directly; the other fractions are added to three pions.
- *1 GeV ≤ W ≤ 3 GeV*: exHad first draws whether the event is $`\pi^+\pi^-`$, three pions, four pions or
  another final state, with these fractions. It generates $`\pi^+\pi^-`$ directly, and the other three
  kinds by Pythia string fragmentation with trial events from the isovector vector ($`\rho`$-like),
  isoscalar vector ($`\omega`$-like) and isovector axial vector ($`a_1`$-like) parts, keeping only events of the
  drawn kind. The fragmentation parameters were fitted to the neutral-current fractions at $`W`$ = 1.00–1.65 GeV
  as for the charged current; above 1.65 GeV they change smoothly towards Pythia's default values, which
  they would reach at 3.5 GeV.
- *3 GeV < W ≤ 5 GeV*: the constrained fragmentation model with $`m_\ast = 3`$ GeV, $`b_F`$ equal to the
  fractions at 3 GeV, and trial events from the $`\rho`$-like, $`\omega`$-like and $`a_1`$-like parts with relative
  weights 0.22, 0.02 and 0.76. The channel groups are two, three and four pions, all with $`T_F = 1`$; exHad
  generates $`\pi^+\pi^-`$ directly with probability $`P_F`$.

#### s s̄ neutral current

The strange part of the neutral current has a vector ($`\phi`$-like) and an axial vector
($`f_1(1420)`$-like) component, whose tabulated fractions of $`K\bar K`$, $`K\bar K\pi`$ and other final states
fix its composition up to $`W = 3`$ GeV. Its strings use the fragmentation parameters fitted to the
fractions of the neutral current, which approach Pythia's default values at 3.5 GeV.

- *W ≤ 3 GeV*: exHad generates $`K\bar K`$ ($`K^+K^-`$ with probability 0.6, $`K^0\bar K^0`$ with 0.4) and,
  above 1.40 GeV, $`K\bar K\pi`$ (as $`K^\ast\bar K`$ and its charge conjugate) directly with their fractions.
  The other final states, present above $`W \approx 2.2`$ GeV, come from $`s\bar s`$ string fragmentation with
  the quantum numbers of the two components, excluding $`K\bar K`$ and $`K\bar K\pi`$.
- *3 GeV < W < 3.75 GeV*: each event comes from $`s\bar s`$ string fragmentation restricted by the quantum
  numbers of the two components, with probability $`t^2(3 - 2t)`$, $`t = (W - 3~\mathrm{GeV})/(0.75~\mathrm{GeV})`$,
  and otherwise from the composition at 3 GeV, generated at 3 GeV and brought to $`W`$ by the common momentum
  factor. The cubic polynomial rises from 0 to 1 with vanishing slope at both ends.
- *W ≥ 3.75 GeV*: $`s\bar s`$ string fragmentation with these restrictions and the component weights at
  3 GeV.

#### u s̄ charged current

At every $`W`$ below 5 GeV, joined to the parton shower description between 4 and 5 GeV, exHad generates the resonances $`K^\ast(892)`$,
$`K_1(1270)`$, $`K_1(1400)`$ and $`K^\ast(1410)`$ and the state $`K\eta`$. Each resonance has a relativistic
Breit–Wigner distribution in $`W`$ with an energy-dependent width, normalized so that its integral with the
kinematic factor of τ decays equals the measured branching ratio of $`\tau^- \to X^-\nu_\tau`$ (Particle Data
Group); $`K\eta`$ uses the line shape of $`K^\ast(1410)`$ with its own τ branching ratio. The resonances decay
into $`K\pi`$, $`K\rho`$ and $`K^\ast\pi`$ with isospin fractions and the momentum dependence $`q^{2L+1}`$ of their
orbital angular momentum $`L`$. The decay into a single kaon, $`N \to K\ell`$, is a separately generated
channel.

#### c d̄ and c s̄ charged currents

Pythia string fragmentation of the $`c\bar d`$ or $`c\bar s`$ pair generates the hadrons, with the isospin test
of [Quantum numbers of the decaying particle](#quantum-numbers-of-the-decaying-particle) for the isospin of
the current (1/2 for $`c\bar d`$, 0 for $`c\bar s`$) and no test of spin, parity, G parity or C parity. Events
whose only primary hadron is a $`D^\pm`$ ($`D_s^\pm`$) meson are rejected, because $`N \to D\ell`$ ($`D_s\ell`$) is a
separately generated channel. The hadrons are generated on a 25 MeV grid in $`W`$ starting 5 MeV above the
$`D\pi`$ ($`DK`$) threshold, and at the exact $`W`$ within 5 MeV of the threshold.

#### Currents with a bottom quark or a charm quark pair

The parton shower description generates the hadrons at every $`W`$ for the neutral currents into $`c\bar c`$ and $`b\bar b`$ and
the charged currents into $`u\bar b`$ and $`c\bar b`$, from $`W`$ = 3.75, 10.56, 5.42 and 7.155 GeV, respectively.

### Accuracy and limitations

The multihadron probabilities of `hnl` at low $`W`$ rest on τ decay spectral functions, which the variations
keep fixed.

- Above $`W = 1.65`$ GeV, where the τ data end, the charged current uses the channel fractions and the
  relative vector and axial vector weights at 1.65 GeV, with the sum of its vector and axial vector
  spectral functions held at its value at 1.65 GeV. For the neutral current, the isovector spectral functions
  are interpolated linearly from their values at 1.65 GeV to a common constant at 2 GeV and held there.
  These are extrapolation assumptions, and the [model variations](#model-variations) do not propagate the
  experimental uncertainties of the spectral functions.
- Just above the $`D\pi`$ threshold ($`c\bar d`$) and the $`DK`$ threshold ($`c\bar s`$), string fragmentation
  rarely produces an allowed state, and requests there fail with an error.
- In narrow mass intervals just below the thresholds of the channels with a bottom quark, between 5.41 and
  10.56 GeV, linear interpolation of the tables leaves these channels branching ratios of at most about
  $`10^{-9}`$; a request in a sample of all decay channels that selects such a channel fails.

## Model variations

The variations change the power $`p`$ and the finite-mass factor of Eq. (2) or, for `dark-photon`, the
fitted coefficients of the electromagnetic cross sections.

| Variation | Models | Change |
|---|---|---|
| `central` | all | the model as described above |
| `p-low` | `alp-fermion`, scalar models, `b-l`, `hnl` | $`p = 1.75`$ in Eq. (2) for all channel groups |
| `p-high` | `alp-fermion`, scalar models, `b-l`, `hnl` | $`p = 2.25`$ in Eq. (2) for all channel groups |
| `saturation-off` | `alp-fermion`, scalar models, `b-l`, `hnl` | $`T_F = 1`$ for all channel groups |
| `coefficient-eigenmode-NN-plus`, `coefficient-eigenmode-NN-minus` | `dark-photon` | the fitted coefficients of the seven cross sections shifted along eigenvector NN = 01–31 of their covariance matrix, in the positive or negative direction |

The variations of $`p`$ and $`T_F`$ act where Eq. (2) enters: for `alp-fermion` above 1.911 GeV, for the scalar
models from 2 GeV, for `b-l` from 2 GeV, and for `hnl` in the $`u\bar d`$ charged current above
$`W = 1.651`$ GeV and in the $`u\bar u`$, $`d\bar d`$ neutral current above $`W = 3`$ GeV. The range 1.75–2.25 of
$`p`$ tests the common-power assumption and has no statistical confidence level. The events of the
parton shower description are unchanged. No variation covers the uncertainties of the exclusive calculations, of the τ spectral
functions, of the charm rates, of the matching intervals, or of the fragmentation parameters of Pythia.
The selection of a variation is described in [INTERFACES.md](INTERFACES.md).

## Numerical inputs

exHad contains its decay rates, fitted coefficients and probabilities of channel groups as tables in `data/`,
and evaluates them at the requested mass for every request.

The tabulated branching ratios, decay lengths and total widths cover the following masses; exHad
generates decays only within the mass ranges of the README model table.

| Model | Tabulated quantities | Masses |
|---|---|---|
| `dark-photon` | branching ratios and decay length $`c\tau`$ | 0.003–23 GeV |
| `b-l` | none; exHad computes the widths | — |
| `alp-fermion` | branching ratios | 0.01–5.2 GeV |
| `scalar-central`, `scalar-lower`, `scalar-upper`, `scalar-1809` | branching ratios and decay length $`c\tau`$ | 0.01–63 GeV |
| `hnl` | branching ratios and total widths | 0.0002–40 GeV |

The other tables contain:

- exclusive widths and fractions of the exclusive calculations, and the coefficients of the fitted
  electromagnetic cross sections with their covariance eigenvectors;
- the measured values of $`R`$ and the exclusive charm cross sections;
- the probabilities $`b_F`$, the nodes of $`T_F`$, the Pythia probabilities $`Q_F`$ and $`q_{i|F}`$ and the source
  probabilities $`r_a`$ of the constrained fragmentation model;
- the τ-derived channel fractions, component weights, fitted string parameters and resonance parameters
  of the `hnl` currents.

The fits and the Pythia samples that produced these tables are not repeated during event generation. At
the requested mass:

- branching ratios and widths are interpolated linearly in mass between the tabulated nodes, without
  extrapolation outside the table;
- every channel without quarks in the `hnl` tables, and every nonhadronic channel of `alp-fermion` and
  the scalar models, has zero rate at and below its threshold, the sum of the masses of its decay
  products, and a rate linear from zero at the threshold to the first tabulated node above it; the
  branching ratios of the open channels are their rates divided by the sum of these rates, and for
  `hnl` the three-neutrino branching ratio remains one minus the sum of the branching ratios of the
  tabulated channels;
- channels with quarks keep plain linear interpolation;
- $`\ln T_F`$, and $`\ln Q_F`$ where $`Q_F`$ is positive at all tabulated masses, are interpolated as monotone
  cubic functions of $`\ln s`$; the source probabilities $`r_a`$ are interpolated linearly in mass;
- the perturbative widths, the form factors of the separately generated `dark-photon` and `b-l` channels
  and the fitted cross sections are evaluated at the requested mass.

## Scope of the description

exHad generates the decays of a particle at rest; its production, lifetime, decay position, polarization and
detector response are outside the package.

- The orientation of an event in the rest frame of the decaying particle carries no information
  about its production. The events of `dark-photon` and `b-l`, and the events that the scalar models
  generate from a charm quark pair, have a quark direction whose density is proportional to
  $`1 + \cos^2\theta`$, where $`\theta`$ is the angle to the $`z`$ axis of the returned event; a host that
  boosts such an event along $`z`$ therefore obtains a correlation between the decay axis and the
  flight direction. All other events are isotropic.
- The upper ends of the mass ranges in the README model table are set by the input calculations and by the
  masses at which the models were tested. The accuracy is not uniform within a range; the accuracy
  subsections of the models describe it.
- A description at higher masses needs new decay rates, hadronic final states for the heavy-flavour
  channels that open, and tests of normalization, thresholds, quantum numbers and momentum conservation.
  Eq. (2) is calibrated at masses of a few GeV and has not been tested as a precision prediction at
  higher masses.
