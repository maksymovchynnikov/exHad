# Sampling of hadronic events from string fragmentation

This document gives the random procedure by which exHad generates the string fragmentation events
of `alp-fermion`, the [scalar models](PHYSICS.md#scalars-mixing-with-the-higgs-boson-scalar-central-scalar-lower-scalar-upper-scalar-1809),
`b-l` and `hnl` (the rejection sampler). It then describes the accelerated sampler, which generates
the [unweighted events](INTERFACES.md#weighted-alp-fermion-samples) of
`alp-fermion`, the scalar models and `b-l` with fewer Pythia fragmentations, the conditions under
which the two samplers agree, and the tests of this agreement. The string fragmentation events of
`dark-photon` are generated by the procedure described in
[PHYSICS.md](PHYSICS.md#hadronic-decay-rates-from-electromagnetic-data-and-exclusive-calculations).

The channel groups $`F`$, their probabilities $`P_F`$, the quantum numbers of the decaying particle
and the weights of rejection sampling are defined in
[PHYSICS.md](PHYSICS.md#generation-of-hadronic-events-by-rejection-sampling). The choice between
string fragmentation and the separately generated channels, and the generation of those
channels, follow the same procedure in both samplers. The request parameter that selects the
sampler is described in [INTERFACES.md](INTERFACES.md#rejection-and-accelerated-samplers).

## Event generation by rejection sampling

The rejection sampler generates a string fragmentation event in the five random steps listed
below, and its accepted events are Pythia strings that pass the quantum number test, reweighted
to the channel probabilities of the model.

These steps generate the string fragmentation events of `alp-fermion`, the scalar models and
`b-l`, and, with the components of the current as sources, those of the `hnl` decays through the
$`u\bar d`$ charged current and the $`u\bar u`$, $`d\bar d`$ neutral current. For
`alp-fermion` and `hnl`, exHad first assigns the event, with probability $`P_F`$, to each channel
group that it generates directly without string fragmentation (3π and ππγ of `alp-fermion`; the
groups named in the [`hnl` currents](PHYSICS.md#hadrons-from-the-quarkantiquark-pair-at-invariant-mass-w)),
and the steps below generate all other events. The steps use the following quantities.

- $`m`$ is the mass of the decaying particle; for `hnl` it is the invariant mass $`W`$ of the
  quark–antiquark pair.
- $`a`$ is the *source* of the string, chosen with probability $`r_a`$ at mass $`m`$: a gluon pair
  $`gg`$ or an $`s\bar s`$ pair for `alp-fermion` and the scalar models, and a light quark pair or an
  $`s\bar s`$ pair for `b-l`. The light quark pair of `b-l` is $`u\bar u`$ or $`d\bar d`$ with equal
  probability, chosen anew for every string. For `hnl` the sources are the components of the
  current listed in [PHYSICS.md](PHYSICS.md#hadrons-from-the-quarkantiquark-pair-at-invariant-mass-w).
- $`x`$ denotes a *trial event*: the primary hadrons, which are the hadrons formed in string
  fragmentation before any of them decays, with their momenta and their decays. $`p_a(x)`$ is the
  probability with which [Pythia](https://pythia.org) 8.317 produces $`x`$ from source $`a`$.
- $`\alpha(x)`$ is the probability that the primary hadrons pass the test of the
  [quantum numbers of the source](PHYSICS.md#quantum-numbers-of-the-decaying-particle). The
  isospin test gives a probability between 0 and 1, and the other tests give 0 or 1.
- $`w(x)`$ is the weight of the trial event: $`w_F`$ of
  [Eq. (4) of PHYSICS.md](PHYSICS.md#generation-of-hadronic-events-by-rejection-sampling) for its
  channel group $`F`$, multiplied by $`f_{i|F}/q_{i|F}`$ for its charge combination $`i`$ where the
  model fixes the charge combinations, with $`q_{i|F}`$ the tabulated probability of combination $`i`$
  among Pythia's trial events in $`F`$. Final states of separately generated channels, of the
  directly generated groups, and two-pion final states of `b-l` have $`w = 0`$. So do events in
  which a decay chain ends in a particle other than a pion, kaon, η, η′, photon or nucleon, for
  example a lepton.
- $`M`$ is the largest of these weights at mass $`m`$, over the channel groups that string
  fragmentation generates, the remainder $`R`$, and the charge combinations within them.

Each event is generated by the following steps.

1. Choose the source $`a`$ with probability $`r_a`$.
2. Place the two partons back to back in their rest frame, sharing the energy $`m`$ equally. The
   direction of the partons is isotropic for `alp-fermion`, the scalar models and `hnl`. For `b-l`
   the density of the quark direction is proportional to $`1 + \cos^2\theta`$, where $`\theta`$ is the
   angle to the z axis. Pythia forms a string between the partons and fragments it into primary
   hadrons. If Pythia fails to fragment the string, repeat this step with the same source $`a`$.
3. Keep the primary hadrons with probability $`\alpha(x)`$. Otherwise return to step 2 with the same
   source $`a`$.
4. Pythia decays the unstable hadrons. Each primary hadron is followed through its decays down to
   the first pion, kaon, η, η′, photon or nucleon; these particles fix the channel group $`F`$ and the
   charge combination $`i`$ ([Widths and channel groups](PHYSICS.md#widths-and-channel-groups)). Where
   the model fixes the charge combinations of $`K\bar K\pi`$ in `alp-fermion`, below 3 GeV, neutral
   kaons are followed to $`K^0_S`$ or $`K^0_L`$. If a decay fails, or if the final state of `b-l` is ηγ,
   which exHad generates separately, return to step 2 with the same source $`a`$.
5. Accept the event with probability $`w/M`$. An event with a final particle whose four-momentum is
   numerically spacelike has $`w = 0`$. After a rejection, return to step 1.

The number of attempts is finite: exHad uses the smallest number of attempts for which the
probability that none of them is accepted stays below $`2^{-64}`$, and a request that exhausts this
number fails with an error.

The accepted events are distributed as

```math
\frac{dN}{dx} \propto \sum_a r_a \frac{p_a(x)\, \alpha(x)}{A_a}\, w(x), \qquad A_a = \sum_x p_a(x)\, \alpha(x),
```

where the sum runs over trial events whose decays succeed and, for `b-l`, whose final state is
not ηγ. $`A_a`$ is the probability that a string from source $`a`$ passes steps 3 and 4. The accepted
events are therefore Pythia strings that pass the quantum number test, combined over the sources
with probabilities $`r_a`$ and reweighted by $`w`$. Among them, each channel group and each charge
combination has the probability given by the model
([PHYSICS.md](PHYSICS.md#generation-of-hadronic-events-by-rejection-sampling)), to the precision
with which the tables $`Q_F`$ and $`q_{i|F}`$ reproduce Pythia. Where exHad generates some channel
groups directly, the probability of each remaining group is $`P_F`$ divided by one minus the total
probability of the directly generated groups.

## Accelerated generation of unweighted events

The accelerated sampler generates events with the distribution of the rejection sampler, using
fewer Pythia fragmentations, in two ways. For the group ηππ of `alp-fermion` and for the group 4π
of the scalar models, it draws the channel first and forces the string breaks to produce it,
keeping Pythia's relative probability of each fragmentation history. For all other channel groups,
it decides at the *final join*, the last flavour decision of a fragmentation attempt, whether to
accept the event, to change the source or to start a new attempt, and it generates decays only
while acceptance is still possible.

The rejection sampler reproduces $`P_F`$ and $`f_{i|F}`$ only as precisely as the tables $`Q_F`$ and
$`q_{i|F}`$ reproduce Pythia. The accelerated sampler draws the group ηππ with $`P_{\eta\pi\pi}`$ and
its four charge combinations with $`f_{i|F}`$, and the group 4π with $`P_{4\pi}`$. The probabilities of
these two groups in the two samplers therefore agree within the statistical precision of these
tables, and the distributions within each charge combination are identical.

The numbers below use the Pythia fragmentation parameters listed in the input files, which are the
same for `alp-fermion`, the scalar models and `b-l`.

### Requests that use the accelerated sampler

With `execution="auto"`, unweighted samples use the accelerated sampler for `alp-fermion` above
2.4 GeV, for the scalar models from 3 to 5 GeV, and for `b-l` from 2 to 5 GeV.

- For `alp-fermion` and the scalar models above 4 GeV, the accelerated sampler generates the
  events that the [transition to showered Pythia](PHYSICS.md#decays-into-quarks-and-gluons-above-4-gev)
  takes from the constrained fragmentation model.
- For `b-l`, it generates the string fragmentation events. The separately generated channels and
  open charm are generated as with the rejection sampler.
- Samples of all decay channels use the same sampler for their hadronic decays.

### Conditions under which fragmentation attempts are independent

Pythia fragments a string in a sequence of attempts, and under the settings listed below a new
attempt has the same distribution as a discarded one, so the accelerated sampler can discard
attempts without changing the distribution of the events.

An attempt chooses the flavours at the string ends (a gluon pair forms a closed string, which is
first cut by a quark–antiquark pair) and then breaks the string, each break at a randomly chosen
end. A string break selects the flavour of a new quark–antiquark pair, the hadron that this pair
forms with the flavour at that string end, and the transverse momentum and momentum fraction of
that hadron. When the energy left in the string is too small for a further break, Pythia combines
the two flavours that the last break leaves at the string ends into one more hadron. This step is
the final join, and it delivers that hadron together with the hadron of the last break. An attempt
with $`n`$ breaks therefore produces $`n+1`$ hadrons: the hadrons of the first $`n-1`$ breaks, each
formed on its own, and then the last two, which are the hadron of the last break and the hadron of
the final join. If the final join fails, Pythia discards the attempt and starts a new attempt from
the same partons.
After 31 failed attempts the hadronization of the event restarts from the same partons, up to ten
times. The partons and their momenta are fixed before fragmentation and do not depend on any later
decision.

The attempts of a string are independent and identically distributed when a failed attempt does
not change the conditions of the next one. This holds under the following conditions.

- The stopping mass `StringFragmentation:stopMass` is zero, so Pythia does not raise it after
  failed attempts.
- The Lund string model is used (`Fragmentation:model = 0`).
- Close packing, rope hadronization, space–time vertices, hadronic rescattering, strange
  junctions, suppression of leading baryons, variations of the fragmentation parameters, and
  the retry of a failed string as a low-mass system are switched off
  (`ClosePacking:doClosePacking`, `Ropewalk:RopeHadronization`, `Ropewalk:doFlavour`,
  `Fragmentation:setVertices`, `HadronLevel:Rescatter`,
  `StringFragmentation:doStrangeJunctions`, `StringFlav:suppressLeadingB`, `VariationFrag:flav`,
  `VariationFrag:z`, `VariationFrag:pT`, `MiniStringFragmentation:tryAfterFailedFrag`).
- For a gluon pair, half the mass $`m`$ exceeds four times `FragmentationSystems:mJoin`, so that
  Pythia never joins nearby partons, which it tries after 10 and 20 failed attempts. With
  `FragmentationSystems:mJoin` = 0.3 GeV this requires $`m > 2.4`$ GeV.
- The constituent masses of the u and d quarks are equal.

The accelerated sampler checks these conditions for the requested mass whenever it starts and
fails with an error if one is violated. Under them, an attempt that is discarded at its final
join, or that fails for any other reason, is replaced by an attempt with the same distribution.

### Direct generation of the group ηππ in `alp-fermion` decays

exHad draws a charge combination of the group ηππ with the probabilities of the `alp-fermion`
model and then forces a gluon string to produce its three mesons, with Pythia's relative
probability of each fragmentation history.

A string fragmentation event of `alp-fermion` that is not assigned to 3π or ππγ belongs to the
group ηππ, which contains ηπ⁺π⁻, ηπ⁰π⁰, η′π⁺π⁻ and η′π⁰π⁰, with probability
$`P_{\eta\pi\pi}/(1 - P_{3\pi} - P_{\pi\pi\gamma})`$. Its charge combination is then drawn with
probability $`f_{i|F}`$ of the
[`alp-fermion` model](PHYSICS.md#charge-combinations-of-ηππ-and-ηππ). Three properties of Pythia's
fragmentation allow these events to be generated directly; the
[installation tests](#tests-of-the-accelerated-sampler-at-installation) verify each of them.

1. A string break produces only pseudoscalar and vector mesons (π, K, η, η′, ρ, $`K^\ast`$, ω, φ)
   or baryons, and every hadron ends in at least one pion, kaon, η, η′, photon or nucleon. A final
   state of three such particles therefore comes from at most three primary hadrons.
2. Among all sets of at most three mesons whose decays can give the chosen final state, only the
   three mesons of the final state themselves pass the quantum number test of `alp-fermion`. For
   example, ηρ⁰ with ρ⁰ → π⁺π⁻ has C parity −1 and fails the test.
3. A string from $`s\bar s`$ cannot fragment into these three mesons.

In the rejection sampler, the events of this group are therefore gluon strings that fragment into
exactly the three mesons of the chosen charge combination. All of them pass the test with the same
probability, 1/3, which is the probability that two pions combine to isospin zero, so the test
does not change their distribution.

The accelerated sampler generates such strings as follows. Each attempt cuts the gluon string by
a u or d quark pair with equal probability. It draws which meson is produced at the first break,
which at the second break and which at the final join, with the relative Pythia probability of
this order. Each of the two string breaks is forced to produce its meson, and the final join is
required to produce the third. Two breaks and a final join give three hadrons: the meson of the
first break is formed on its own, and the mesons of the second break and of the final join are the
last two hadrons of the attempt. Each break occurs at a randomly chosen end of the string, and
Pythia chooses the momentum fractions, transverse momenta and hadron masses as usual.

Along histories with u and d quarks, Pythia's flavour probabilities do not depend on whether a
quark is u or d. The orders therefore differ in probability only at the final join, where Pythia
retries a rejected flavour combination, for example a suppressed η, up to ten times; exHad
evaluates these probabilities from the initialized Pythia parameters. The signs of the two pion
charges are fixed at the first break that produces a charged pion, according to the flavour of the
string end; both sign assignments have the same probability.

An attempt that cannot complete its order fails at the final join, and Pythia starts a new
attempt. When the flavour of a string end does not fit the next meson, or when string energy
remains after two breaks, the next break is given a b quark, so that Pythia proceeds directly to
the final join. This requires the product of `StringFragmentation:stopNewFlav`, the constituent
mass of the b quark and 1 − `StringFragmentation:stopSmear` to exceed $`m`$, which the sampler
checks at start.

Pythia decays the completed strings. An event whose decays fail, whose decay chains do not all end
in pions, kaons, η, η′, photons or nucleons, or that contains a numerically spacelike particle is
generated again. The rejection sampler never accepts such events in this charge combination. Each
completed history thus occurs with its Pythia probability, conditioned on the three mesons, which
is the distribution of this charge combination in the rejection sampler.

### Direct generation of four pions in decays of the scalar models

For the scalar models, exHad generates the group 4π by forcing gluon strings to produce π and ρ
mesons, with the Pythia probability of each fragmentation history multiplied by the probability
that the quantum number test accepts its primary hadrons.

A string fragmentation event of a scalar model belongs to the group 4π with probability
$`P_{4\pi}`$. Three properties allow these events to be generated directly; the
[installation tests](#tests-of-the-accelerated-sampler-at-installation) verify the second and the
third, and the sampler checks the first at start.

1. Every primary hadron ends in at least one pion, kaon, η, η′, photon or nucleon, so four pions
   come from at most four primary hadrons. With zero stopping mass, a string with u, d or s quark
   ends cannot use up its energy at its first break when $`m`$ exceeds 2.4 GeV, a limit set by the
   constituent mass of the s quark and by `StringFragmentation:stopNewFlav` and
   `StringFragmentation:stopSmear`. Every string at these masses therefore contains at least three
   hadrons.
2. The sets of at least three mesons whose decays can give exactly π⁺π⁻π⁺π⁻, π⁺π⁻π⁰π⁰ or
   π⁰π⁰π⁰π⁰ and that pass the quantum number test of the scalar models are π⁺π⁻π⁺π⁻, π⁺π⁻π⁰π⁰,
   π⁰π⁰π⁰π⁰, π⁺π⁻ρ⁰, π⁺π⁰ρ⁻ and π⁻π⁰ρ⁺. The test accepts them with probabilities 1/5 for
   π⁺π⁻π⁺π⁻ and π⁰π⁰π⁰π⁰, 2/15 for π⁺π⁻π⁰π⁰, and 1/6 for the three sets with a ρ meson.
3. No string break or final join with an s quark produces a π or ρ meson. Strings from
   $`s\bar s`$, and gluon strings cut by an s quark pair, therefore never produce these sets.

The accelerated sampler keeps Pythia's cut of the gluon string. Each attempt then draws the meson
types (π⁰, ρ⁰, charged π or charged ρ) of one of these sets, together with the type formed at the
final join. The probability of this draw is proportional to Pythia's flavour probability of one
order of the string breaks and of the meson formed at the final join, multiplied by the number of
distinct orders and by the acceptance of the set. The attempt then draws one of these orders with
equal probability, and each string break is forced to produce the next meson type. A charged π or
ρ meson takes the charge allowed by the flavour of the string end, positive at a u quark or d̄
antiquark end; π⁺π⁰ρ⁻ and π⁻π⁰ρ⁺, which have the same acceptance, arise in this way from one set
of types. Pythia chooses the momentum fractions, transverse momenta and masses as usual.

Pythia's probabilities of producing π⁰, ρ⁰, a charged π or a charged ρ at a break from a u or d
end, and at a final join, do not depend on the flavour of the end or on its side of the string.
The sampler checks this at start, together with the condition on
`StringFragmentation:stopNewFlav` given for the group ηππ. Each completed history therefore occurs
with probability proportional to its Pythia probability times its acceptance, as in the rejection
sampler.

An attempt that cannot complete the drawn set, because the string was cut by an s quark or diquark
pair or because string energy remains after the forced breaks, is given a b quark at its next
break and fails at the final join. After Pythia's decays, events whose final state is not exactly
four pions, for example after ρ⁰ → π⁰γ, are generated again. The rejection sampler assigns such
events to other channel groups, which the accelerated sampler generates as described in the next
subsection.

### Acceptance at the final join for all other channel groups

For all other channel groups, each fragmentation attempt that reaches its final join draws one
uniform random number that decides between acceptance, a change of source and a new attempt, with
probabilities proportional to those of the rejection sampler.

This procedure generates the channel groups of `alp-fermion` other than ηππ, 3π and ππγ, those of
the scalar models other than 4π, and all channel groups of `b-l`. It reproduces the rejection
sampler in which the weights of the directly drawn groups are zero, with $`M`$ the largest of the
remaining weights.

At the final join of an attempt from source $`a`$, all primary hadrons are known, and with them
$`\alpha`$. The sampler draws a uniform random number $`\zeta`$ between 0 and 1. Let $`w`$ be the weight
of the event after its decays, $`\bar r_a = 1 - r_a`$ the probability of the other source, $`K_a`$ a
positive constant called the bound of source $`a`$, and $`D`$ the ratio of the probability with which
the attempt proposes this history to its Pythia probability. $`D = 1`$ when no forced isosinglet
attempts, defined below, are used. The attempt

- is accepted if $`\zeta < \alpha w/(K_a D)`$;
- ends the use of source $`a`$ and continues with the other source if
  $`\alpha w/(K_a D) \le \zeta < \alpha[w + \bar r_a (M - w)]/(K_a D)`$;
- is discarded otherwise, and Pythia starts a new attempt from the same source.

For a string with history $`x`$ from source $`a`$, the rejection sampler accepts it with probability
$`p_a(x)\, \alpha(x)\, w(x)/M`$ and continues with the other source with probability
$`p_a(x)\, \alpha(x)\, \bar r_a [M - w(x)]/M`$: the string passes the test, is rejected, and the
other source is drawn. All other outcomes lead to a new string from the same source. An attempt of
the accelerated sampler proposes $`x`$ with probability proportional to $`D\, p_a(x)`$, with a factor
that depends only on $`a`$. It therefore accepts $`x`$ with probability proportional to
$`p_a(x)\, \alpha(x)\, w(x)/K_a`$ and changes the source with probability proportional to
$`p_a(x)\, \alpha(x)\, \bar r_a [M - w(x)]/K_a`$. These are the probabilities of the rejection sampler
multiplied by a factor that depends only on the source $`a`$, so acceptance and change of source
occur in the ratio of the rejection sampler. Discarded attempts are replaced by attempts with the
same distribution
([Conditions under which fragmentation attempts are independent](#conditions-under-which-fragmentation-attempts-are-independent)).
The first attempt that ends in acceptance or in a change of source therefore has the distribution
of the rejection sampler, and so do the accepted events and the sequence of sources.

The weight $`w`$ is known only after the decays, and both limits grow with $`w`$. The sampler therefore
first compares $`\zeta`$ with the second limit evaluated at the largest weight still possible for the
primary hadrons. This largest weight is $`M`$ in general and 0 for primary hadrons that contain a
baryon in `alp-fermion` and the scalar models, whose final states with baryons have zero weight.
If $`\zeta`$ is above this limit, the attempt is discarded without decays. Otherwise the decays
proceed in two stages. Pythia first decays the short-lived resonances, keeping pions, kaons, η,
η′, nucleons, hyperons and muons undecayed. This fixes the channel group and charge combination
and bounds $`w`$ once more. Pythia then decays the remaining unstable particles. The weight of the fully decayed event never exceeds these bounds; a
violation stops the request with an error.

No limit may exceed 1, which requires $`\alpha[w + \bar r_a (M - w)] \le K_a D`$ for every possible
event. Since $`w + \bar r_a (M - w) \le M`$, the bound $`K_a = M`$ always suffices; exHad uses it for
$`s\bar s`$ strings of `alp-fermion` and the scalar models. For gluon strings, a smaller bound
follows from three properties, and a smaller bound means fewer discarded attempts.

- **Isospin.** The decaying particle has total isospin zero. In a set of primary hadrons, let $`j`$
  be the largest isospin of any hadron of the set. For the complete state to have isospin zero, the
  remaining hadrons must combine to isospin $`j`$, with third component opposite to that of the
  hadron of isospin $`j`$, and that coupling has the squared Clebsch–Gordan coefficient $`1/(2j + 1)`$.
  Hence $`\alpha \le 1/2`$ for every set that contains a hadron of nonzero isospin.
- **Baryons.** In `alp-fermion` and the scalar models a set with a baryon has $`w = 0`$, so its
  second limit is at most $`r_{s\bar s} M/(K_{gg} D)`$. The probability $`r_{s\bar s}`$ of the
  $`s\bar s`$ source is at most 1/2, which the sampler checks.
- **Isosinglet mesons.** The remaining sets with $`\alpha > 1/2`$ consist only of the isosinglet
  mesons η, η′, ω and φ. With probability $`\beta`$, an attempt forces every string break and the
  final join to produce isosinglet mesons, each chosen with its relative Pythia probability; such
  an attempt is called a *forced isosinglet attempt*. A history of $`n`$ isosinglet mesons from a
  string end of flavour $`q`$ then has $`D = 1 - \beta + \beta/c_n(q)`$, and every other history has
  $`D = 1 - \beta`$. Here $`c_n(q) = t_{\rm break}(q)^{n-1}\, t_{\rm join}(q)`$ is Pythia's probability
  that all $`n`$ mesons are isosinglets, $`t_{\rm break}(q)`$ is the probability that a completed
  string break at an end of flavour $`q`$ produces an isosinglet meson, and $`t_{\rm join}(q)`$ is the
  probability that the final join of ends $`q`$ and $`\bar q`$ produces one, within Pythia's ten tries.
  With $`\beta/(1 - \beta) = 2\max_q c_3(q)`$ and at least three hadrons per string,
  $`D \ge 2(1 - \beta)`$ for all histories of isosinglet mesons. A string of mesons contains at
  least three of them above 2.4 GeV for exHad's fragmentation parameters. The sampler checks this
  at start for the scalar models; for `alp-fermion`, a string with fewer hadrons would give a limit
  above 1, which stops the request with an error.

These three properties give the bound $`K_{gg} = M/[2(1 - \beta)]`$ for gluon strings. For exHad's
fragmentation parameters, $`t_{\rm break}(u) = t_{\rm break}(d) = 0.138`$,
$`t_{\rm join}(u) = t_{\rm join}(d) = 0.376`$, $`t_{\rm break}(s) = 0.0540`$ and
$`t_{\rm join}(s) = 0.9997`$, which give $`\beta = 0.0142`$. The sampler evaluates $`t_{\rm break}`$ and
$`t_{\rm join}`$ from the initialized Pythia flavour parameters. A limit above 1 at any decision stops
the request with an error.

### Light and strange quark strings of `b-l`

For `b-l`, exHad draws no channel group in advance. Each event is decided when its fragmentation
attempt is complete: at the final join, except for the $`s\bar s`$ string at 2.0 GeV, which has no
final join and is decided on its completed set of primary hadrons (below). Pythia's $`u\bar u`$ and
$`d\bar d`$ strings differ slightly, so the light quark pair uses one of two constructions that keep
their relative weight exact, and each source has its own bound.

The quark current of `b-l` has isospin zero, so $`\alpha \le 1/2`$ for every set with a hadron of
nonzero isospin, as for gluon strings. A set of two or more isosinglet mesons never belongs to the
group 3π, because each such meson either remains an η or η′ or decays into at least two pions or
into a final state that contains a particle other than a pion. A set with baryons belongs either
to the remainder $`R`$, with weight $`w_R`$, or to the separately generated nucleon–antinucleon
channels, with weight 0. Let $`w_{\max}`$ be the largest weight of all groups other than 3π. Before
decays, the largest weight still possible is therefore $`w_R`$ for sets with baryons and $`w_{\max}`$
for sets of at least two isosinglet mesons. Without forced isosinglet attempts, all limits stay at
or below 1 with

```math
K_a = \max\left[\frac{M}{2},\ w_{\max} + \bar r_a (M - w_{\max})\right].
```

**Light quark pair.** The rejection sampler chooses $`u\bar u`$ or $`d\bar d`$ anew for every string.
Pythia uses different masses within isospin multiplets, for example for K⁺ and K⁰ or for p and n,
so $`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$ strings differ slightly, and so do their probabilities of completing an
attempt and of passing the quantum number test. Discarding an attempt while keeping the quark
flavour would change the relative weight of $`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$ strings. exHad uses one of two
constructions that reproduce the rejection sampler exactly. In both, $`a`$ denotes the light quark
pair and $`\bar r_a`$ the probability of the $`s\bar s`$ source.

- **Complete strings.** No attempt is discarded at its final join. An attempt whose outcome is
  "discard" completes its string without decays, and the next string chooses its flavour anew.
  The bound is $`K_a`$ given above.
- **Flavour chain.** The flavour of the light quark pair is kept from one string to the next and
  is changed by an explicit decision. At the final join, the attempt is accepted if
  $`\zeta < \alpha w/(K_a D)`$, changes to $`s\bar s`$ if
  $`\zeta < \alpha[w + \bar r_a (M - w)]/(K_a D)`$, changes the flavour of the light quark pair for
  the next string if $`\zeta < (M + \alpha [w + \bar r_a (M - w)])/(2 K_a D)`$, and is otherwise
  discarded, after which Pythia starts a new attempt with the same flavour. The third interval has
  the width $`(M - \alpha [w + \bar r_a (M - w)])/(2 K_a D)`$. This is half the probability with which
  the rejection sampler starts a new light quark string, because a new random flavour equals the
  old one half of the time. The probability that an attempt of a given flavour is completed
  multiplies all three outcomes alike and cancels. The construction reproduces the rejection
  sampler up to the difference between $`u\bar u`$ and $`d\bar d`$ strings in the probability that
  Pythia fails all 310 attempts at a string, which is below $`10^{-30}`$. It allows discarded
  attempts and forced isosinglet attempts with $`\beta/(1 - \beta) = c_2(u)/3`$, which give
  $`K_a = 0.75 M/(1 - \beta) = 0.763 M`$ below 2.23 GeV, twice the Λ mass $`m_\Lambda`$, where pairs of
  isosinglet baryons become possible. Above $`2m_\Lambda`$, sets such as ΛΛ̄ require the larger bound
  $`K_a = \max(0.75 M,\ [M + w_R + \bar r_a (M - w_R)]/2)/(1 - \beta)`$.

At each mass the light quark pair uses the construction with the smaller bound. For the weights of
`b-l` in all variations, this is the flavour chain below $`2m_\Lambda`$ and complete strings above.

**Strange quark pair.** With fixed $`s`$ and $`\bar s`$ ends, the attempts of a string are identically
distributed, because a string between two quarks never joins additional partons. The sampler
therefore discards attempts, and forced isosinglet attempts with
$`\beta/(1 - \beta) = c_2(s) = 0.054`$ give $`D \ge 2(1 - \beta)`$ for all histories of at least two
isosinglet mesons. The resulting bound is $`K_a = M/[2(1 - \beta)] = 0.527 M`$ below $`2m_\Lambda`$ and
$`K_a = \max(M/2,\ w_R + \bar r_a (M - w_R))/(1 - \beta)`$ above it, where $`\bar r_a`$ is the
probability of the light quark source. The strange quark pair uses whichever of this bound and the
bound without forced isosinglet attempts is smaller.

**Hyperons.** The first decay stage keeps hyperons undecayed, so the channel group of a set with a
hyperon is known only after the second stage. For such sets, only the bound from the primary
hadrons applies before the full decays.

**Strange quark pair at 2.0 GeV.** At $`m = 2.0`$ GeV the mass of the $`s\bar s`$ pair exceeds the
constituent masses of the two quarks by exactly `HadronLevel:mStringMin` = 1 GeV. Pythia then
fragments it as a low-mass system, which forms its hadrons without a final join. The sampler
decides on the complete set of primary hadrons with $`K_a = M`$.

## Tests of the accelerated sampler at installation

`tools/configure.py` builds the accelerated sampler in every installation and, before enabling
it, tests the properties of the installed Pythia library on which the constructions above rely;
the configuration fails if any test fails.

The accelerated sampler calls the installed Pythia library through its public interfaces for
fragmentation models, flavour selection and user hooks (`FragmentationModel`, `StringFlav` and
`UserHooks`) and leaves the library unmodified. The Pythia settings listed in
[Conditions under which fragmentation attempts are independent](#conditions-under-which-fragmentation-attempts-are-independent)
are checked each time the sampler starts. The tests at installation are the following.

- **Pythia files.** SHA-256 checksums are compared with those of the Pythia8.317 files whose code
  the constructions above rely on: four headers present in every installation, which declare the
  interfaces for fragmentation models and user hooks and the classes for string fragmentation and
  for the selection of flavour, transverse momentum and momentum fraction; and, where the Pythia
  directory contains its source files, the five source files that implement string fragmentation,
  that selection, the fragmentation model interface, the hadron level and the main Pythia class. A
  missing header, or a file that differs from its checksum, stops the configuration and names the
  file. A Pythia installation without source files is tested by the following tests alone.
- **Mesons from string breaks.** With exHad's fragmentation parameters, the mesons that a string
  break can produce are exactly π, K, η, η′, ρ, $`K^\ast`$, ω and φ.
- **The group ηππ.** For each of ηπ⁺π⁻, ηπ⁰π⁰, η′π⁺π⁻ and η′π⁰π⁰, all sets of at most three such
  mesons whose Pythia decay channels can produce exactly this final state are enumerated. Only the
  set of the three mesons of the final state passes the quantum number test of `alp-fermion`, and
  no sequence of two breaks and a final join of an $`s\bar s`$ string produces it.
- **Four pions.** For π⁺π⁻π⁺π⁻, π⁺π⁻π⁰π⁰ and π⁰π⁰π⁰π⁰, all sets of at most four such mesons are
  enumerated. The sets of at least three mesons that pass the quantum number test of the scalar
  models are exactly the six sets listed in
  [Direct generation of four pions in decays of the scalar models](#direct-generation-of-four-pions-in-decays-of-the-scalar-models),
  and no string break or final join with an s quark produces a π or ρ meson.
- **Flavour probabilities.** For string ends of flavour u, d and s, $`2\times10^7`$ string breaks and
  final joins drawn by Pythia's own flavour selection agree within five standard deviations with
  the probabilities that the sampler computes: that a break yields a hadron, that a break produces
  each isosinglet meson, and that a final join produces each isosinglet meson; and, for u and d
  ends, that a break or a final join produces a π⁰, ρ⁰, charged π or charged ρ meson.
- **Unconstrained strings.** Without forced flavours and without decisions at the final join, the
  fragmentation of the accelerated sampler reproduces unmodified Pythia hadron by hadron for the
  same random numbers: 20,000 $`gg`$ and 20,000 $`s\bar s`$ strings at 3.6 GeV, and 20,000 $`u\bar u`$
  and 20,000 $`s\bar s`$ strings of `b-l` at 2.0 and 3.6 GeV. At 2.0 GeV the $`s\bar s`$ pair is
  fragmented as a low-mass system.
- **Random number initialization.** The accelerated sampler initializes Pythia's random number
  generator with a faster evaluation of Pythia's own seed procedure. For about 32,000 seeds it
  reproduces Pythia's generator state bit for bit, together with the next 128 random numbers. The
  faster evaluation exists in a version that uses the vector instructions of the processor and in
  a version without them; complete `alp-fermion` events generated with the two versions are
  identical at nine combinations of mass, variation and seed.

`.runtime/accelerator/build.json` records the checked files and the results of all tests. If a
test fails, the configuration stops and exHad generates no events until a configuration succeeds.

## Statistical comparisons with the rejection sampler

Two-sample tests of channel groups, charge combinations, multiplicities and momentum
distributions, with Holm correction at familywise level 0.01, found no significant difference
between the accelerated sampler and the rejection sampler at any tested mass and variation. The
group ηππ of `alp-fermion` was not compared in this way.

Each comparison generates independent samples with the two samplers at one mass and variation and
applies a set of two-sample tests to the same observables. Holm's step-down correction adjusts the
p values of the set so that the probability of rejecting equality in any test of the set, if the
two distributions are equal, is at most 1%. Below, $`z`$ denotes a difference in units of its
standard error.

**`b-l`.** Samples of 100,000 events from each sampler were compared at 2.0 GeV, where the light
quark pair uses the flavour chain, at 2.2 and 3.0 GeV in `central`, at 2.1 GeV in `p-high`, and at
4.5 GeV in all four variations. Each comparison used 21 tests: the numbers of particles, charged
particles, strange particles, photons and baryons; the composition of the final states and the
number of each particle species; the energy and transverse momentum of the leading particle; the
total energies of charged particles and of photons; and the invariant mass of the leading π⁺π⁻
pair. The smallest corrected p value was 0.31. At 2.0 GeV, samples of 200,000 events agree in the
mean numbers of particles, charged particles, photons and kaons within $`|z| < 1.3`$. Decaying every
completed string, including those that the sampler discards before decays, gave no event whose
weight exceeds its bounds from the primary hadrons or from the short-lived decays, in 4.1 million
strings between 2.0 and 5.0 GeV in all variations.

**Scalar models.** For `scalar-central`, four comparisons each used 11 tests of channel groups,
sources, compositions of the final states, multiplicities and momentum distributions. The group 4π
was compared with the rejection sampler in which only 4π has a nonzero weight, at 3.5 GeV (100,000
events of the accelerated sampler and 32,000 of the rejection sampler) and at 4.5 GeV (30,000 and
6,000 events). The other groups were compared with the rejection sampler in which the weight of 4π
is zero, at 3.5 and 4.5 GeV (100,000 events each). No test rejected equality. For 4π events at
3.0 GeV (60,000 and 16,000 events), the sets of primary hadrons agree (χ² p value 0.48), with a
fraction of sets of three hadrons of 0.4844 and 0.4854 ($`z = -0.21`$). At 4.0 GeV the fraction of 4π
among string fragmentation events agrees with $`P_{4\pi}`$ within $`|z| \le 1.4`$ and with the
rejection sampler within $`|z| \le 0.9`$ in all four variations.

**`alp-fermion`.** For the channel groups other than ηππ, 3π and ππγ, 27 tests of the same kinds
compared the accelerated sampler with the rejection sampler in which the weights of ηππ are zero,
at 3.0, 3.6, 4.0 (all four variations), 4.4 and 5.0 GeV, with 90,000–300,000 events per sample. At
5.0 GeV, above the
[transition to showered Pythia](PHYSICS.md#decays-into-quarks-and-gluons-above-4-gev), the comparison
used the string fragmentation events of both samplers. No test rejected equality. At 3.6 GeV, in
samples of 300,000 events, events in which all primary hadrons are isosinglet mesons, the histories
enhanced by forced isosinglet attempts, make up 0.554% and 0.561% of the samples ($`z = -0.35`$). A
sampler that uses the bound of gluon strings without forced isosinglet attempts gives 0.313%
($`z = -14.6`$), so the comparison detects an incorrect treatment of these histories. For the group
ηππ, the installation tests verify that the
[construction](#direct-generation-of-the-group-ηππ-in-alp-fermion-decays) includes every
fragmentation history of these final states.

The equivalence follows from the constructions of the accelerated sampler; the comparisons test
their implementation at the stated masses and variations.
